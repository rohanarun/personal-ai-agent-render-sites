Save. Read. Investigate.

Raindrop.io, Readwise Reader and Super Powers AI solve overlapping but different research problems.

You do not need three subscriptions merely because you have many tabs. First identify what breaks: finding a source again, understanding it, or answering a question that requires more evidence. This is a workflow-based shortlist, not a benchmark or an overall "best app" ranking. We publish Super, so our recommendation must earn its place through a clear use case.

Raindrop.io: start with a source library

Raindrop organizes bookmarks into collections and tags, with multiple views and highlighting. It is a sensible first evaluation when you keep losing useful links or need a structured collection to revisit. Saving a source does not mean endorsing its claims. Official product page.

The Free plan lists unlimited bookmarks, collections, highlights and devices, plus sharing and import/export. Pro adds full-text search, web archive, AI Assistant and AI Suggestions, among other features. Do not assume archiving and full-text search are free, or describe Raindrop as having no AI. The fetched pricing page did not expose a dependable paid amount, so verify your currency and billing term at checkout. Plan details.

Trial: save five sources for one question, add a short reason for keeping each, then try to retrieve the most useful one the following day. If that fixes the problem, stop there rather than buying another system.

Readwise Reader: start with close reading

Reader brings articles, PDFs, EPUBs, newsletters and other reading together with highlighting. Its page describes Ghostreader for questions and explanations, and an MCP server for assistant access to the library. It is not simply a static reading queue. The official FAQ lists a 30-day trial, then a full Readwise subscription at $9.99 per month billed annually or $12.99 monthly. Reader capabilities and pricing FAQ.

Trial: read one important source fully. Save the passage supporting a claim, the author's qualification and your own unresolved question as separate notes. Later, test whether you can recover all three without rereading everything. An AI summary is not a substitute for a passage whose exact meaning matters.

Choose this direction when you already possess the material but do not turn it into usable understanding. Do not move a whole archive before checking whether your actual document formats, export needs and reading habits work well in the trial.

Super Powers AI: start with a bounded investigation

Super publicly describes browser and cloud-computer capabilities. A proposed workflow is investigating a specific question against supplied sources, finding missing primary evidence and drafting a cited comparison. That differs from choosing a long-term bookmark or reading home. No Raindrop or Readwise connection was tested here. Super product description.

Trial: ask for the official prerequisites for two software tools you are evaluating. Supply the scope and ask for a source URL, checked date, exact requirement and unresolved ambiguity for each. Inspect the original pages before making a decision. Do not ask the assistant to invent an overall score from incomparable claims.

Check the account's current charges and plan terms before use. This article does not establish lower cost, better factual accuracy or a faster outcome than either alternative. If your existing library's AI features already answer your question with adequate evidence, an additional assistant may be unnecessary.

Pick the failure you want to fix

The output is an editorial trial suggestion, not an automated product evaluation. No account is connected and no purchase is made.

Use a small evidence chain
  1. Capture the question before the links. "Which tool supports our file format?" is actionable. "Research everything" is not. Record who needs the answer and which constraints came from them.
  2. Keep source and interpretation separate. A bookmark locates a page, a highlight identifies a passage, and your note explains its relevance. Preserve all three when the distinction matters. Never turn a marketing claim into a measured result by rephrasing it.
  3. Resolve freshness explicitly. For changing details such as pricing or supported integrations, revisit the primary page. An archived copy is useful historical evidence, not proof of the current offer.
  4. Export before expanding. Take your trial notes out of the app and check that another person can trace the conclusion. If the chain is unclear, adding more sources usually increases the cleanup burden.
What the cache does, and does not, prove
The public computer-use-cache repository is implementation evidence for response reuse. The pinned proxy source keys normalized requests, stores eligible successful JSON responses and supports expiration and bypass behavior. A hit can avoid another upstream model call; a miss goes upstream.
A cached response is not a freshly visited source, a verified conclusion or proof that a computer action succeeded. This source review does not establish production hit rates, total savings or every deployed setting. For a dated research claim, retain the actual source-check evidence regardless of whether model output was reused.
Buy for the bottleneck, not the bundle
Our editorial starting points are Raindrop for organizing links, Reader for reading and annotations, and a bounded Super trial for open investigation. These are overlapping roles, not exclusive capabilities. Start with one real question and retain a tool only if its output survives your evidence check.
