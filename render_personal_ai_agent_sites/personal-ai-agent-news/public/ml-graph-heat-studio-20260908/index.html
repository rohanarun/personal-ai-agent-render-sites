Laplacian L = D - W

Continuous time, local exchange

Graph heat follows dx/dt = -L x for the unnormalized Laplacian L = D - W. Each edge transfers signal according to the difference at its endpoints. The matrix-exponential solution is x(t) = exp(-t L) x(0). Here it is evaluated through a symmetric eigendecomposition, not Euler time steps.

In a Laplacian eigenvector direction with eigenvalue lambda, heat multiplies the initial coefficient by exp(-t lambda). Larger eigenvalues decay faster. Zero modes remain unchanged, which preserves the average within each connected component. This is a graph low-pass filter: variation is smoothed according to connectivity rather than geometric distance.

A bottleneck is not a barrier

At bridge weight zero without the extra edge, A-B and C-D are separate pairs. Starting at A leaves C and D at zero; the connected pair approaches one-half at each node. Any positive bridge eventually connects all four, so the unit pulse approaches one-quarter at every node. A small positive bridge may still look disconnected over a short observation window.

The displayed mass should remain one and the energy x^T L x should not increase with time on a fixed graph. Changing an edge recomputes the entire trajectory from the original pulse; it does not continue a simulation on a modified graph. Time is in abstract units. This four-node example illustrates signal smoothing, not a trained graph neural network or an empirical prediction. Tiny signed rounding errors can appear near zero in exported values.
