Same set, different first impression

At depth d, take the first d documents from each list. Count the IDs in both prefixes and divide by d. This lesson calls that value agreement at depth. For the reversed example, agreement is zero at depths one and two, one third at depth three, three quarters at depth four, and one at depth five.

Agreement can decrease between depths. After matching A, swap the second and third documents in B: the first result still agrees, depth-two agreement falls to one half, and depth-three agreement returns to one.

Full-depth agreement is always one here because both rankings are permutations of the same five unique IDs. Agreement does not tell you which document is relevant, whether a generated answer is correct, or which retriever is better. Those questions need evidence beyond list overlap.

Beyond this example

Rank-biased overlap is a top-weighted ranking-similarity measure that can work with prefixes and partially shared items. Corsi and Urbano examine how ties affect it. This small lesson computes only per-depth agreement, not RBO; it excludes ties, unequal lengths and missing documents.
