One ticket. Three different kinds of evidence.

Consider a fictional billing-access ticket. At 09:00 the customer reports a charge and failed login. At 09:03 an investigator sees a successful payment in a billing system. The only entitlement record available was captured yesterday. Payment success does not establish that the right account has access now.

Customer statement

“I paid and cannot sign in.”

A report of the problem. Preserve it as a claim, without treating it as a confirmed diagnosis.

Observed billing record

A useful observation, provided the customer, account and transaction match. It is not evidence of entitlement delivery.

Yesterday's entitlement snapshot cannot explain today's login attempt. Read the authorized current record or escalate the missing evidence.

A pilot that can fail honestly

Begin with redacted, read-only cases and an approved list of accounts and work surfaces. Use a stable API or the helpdesk's existing connector when it covers the job. Consider general computer use only for a demonstrated coverage gap, not as a reason to replace a functioning integration.

  1. Define the deliverable. An internal evidence brief with source reference, observation time, account match, unresolved questions and a proposed next step. No automatic customer promises.
  2. Include adverse cases. A wrong-account match, expired session, stale record, conflicting records and an inaccessible source should produce a visible limitation or escalation, not a guessed answer.
  3. Review the result against source records. Record missing or incorrect claims, investigator review time and whether anyone had to redo the work. Compare the same cases with your current process before claiming savings.
  4. Separate permissions from prompts. Read-only access and approval controls must be enforced by the connected systems where available. Instructions alone are not an access-control boundary.

Super's public product describes browser, cloud-app and sandbox work surfaces. That is a basis for evaluating a cross-app workflow, not proof that this billing/helpdesk combination ships as a supported integration. Super product scope

Actual cache technology. A specific boundary.

The public computer-use-cache repository implements an OpenAI-compatible proxy. Successful JSON responses can be stored and reused for matching canonical requests. The implementation includes model, messages, tools and most request-body fields in the matching input; a miss goes upstream. Cache bypass and expiry controls are available.

This is inspectable software, not a benchmark claim. The pinned implementation lets an engineer review the mechanism. A hit can avoid an upstream model call. It does not click through the support system, refresh the customer's entitlement, guarantee task completion, or remove infrastructure and review costs.

In this ticket, freshness wins

Do not strip changing account data from a request just to increase cache hits. A response based on yesterday's observation remains based on yesterday's observation. Re-read the relevant source and keep the observation time with the evidence. Reusing a response is not the same as revalidating the world.

Bottom line

Choose a dedicated support platform when it already handles your customer workflow and integrations. Evaluate Super Powers AI for a specific evidence-gathering gap, with read-only scope and a human-reviewed handoff. The useful question is not which agent sounds smarter. It is which workflow produces the missing, current evidence with an acceptable failure path.
