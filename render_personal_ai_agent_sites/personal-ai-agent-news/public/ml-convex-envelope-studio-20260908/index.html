A minimal convex enclosure

The convex hull is the smallest convex region containing the training points. It includes every straight segment between any two of its points. Interior observations need not become vertices: in the square example, removing the center point would leave the hull unchanged.

The dashed box uses independent minimum and maximum coordinates. It can include locations outside the hull because it ignores relationships between coordinates. In the triangle example, the probe at (6, 6) lies inside the box but outside the triangular hull.

One extra point changes the story

The reset probe (6, 6) is outside the square. Including the extra training point at (8, 8) brings it inside the hull, without collecting observations near that probe. An extreme observation can therefore admit a large previously excluded region.

A toy novelty rule could flag points outside this envelope, but geometric membership is not a probability, a density estimate, or a validated anomaly score. A hollow or multimodal sample can enclose empty space. All points here are synthetic; no coverage or real-world detection guarantee is claimed.

Boundary points are reported separately using a small numerical tolerance. When the sample is collinear, its hull has zero area and this lab reports no 2D region. A lower-dimensional membership test would be a different model.
