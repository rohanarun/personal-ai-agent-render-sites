Which file is approved?

A useful design handoff needs more than a shared folder. Compare Super Powers AI and Dropbox without confusing preparation, delivery and approval.

For a freelance designer handing a campaign to a client, “final-v3-really-final” is a warning sign. The receiver needs the exact revision, its intended use, the reviewer and the decision that applies to it. A download link answers only one of those questions.

Keep the file system. Improve the handoff.

Dropbox: files and access

Dropbox supports shared links and file collaboration. Its sharing overview describes controlling access and working with shared files. Use it when storage, delivery and permissions are the core problem.

It is not just a passive folder: the plan comparison includes version history and Dropbox Replay review features. Check your plan before adding another review tool. A shared link still does not, by itself, document your client's sign-off.

Super's product page describes cloud apps, browsers, research and reports. A proposed trial is turning approved project notes into a delivery brief with explicit missing decisions.

Write a revision-specific handoff

These are illustrative inputs. The note records your statements; it cannot inspect the file, check access or authenticate approval.

Local-only note assembly. No file uploads, account connections or AI calls. Reload resets the example. Keep private links and client secrets out of this sample.


Make the revision, not the filename, the unit of approval
  1. Identify the deliverable. Record its revision and intended use. A review PDF and a production source file can be different deliverables even when they look alike.
  2. Ask for a specific decision. “Please review” leaves the receiver guessing. Name the open question, the person who owns it and the expected next step.
  3. Preserve the decision reference. Keep the approval message or review record alongside the revision it covers. Do not carry approval forward silently after changing that revision.
  4. Test the recipient's access. Verify the intended permission using an appropriate test account. Your ability to open a link does not prove the client's access.
Illustrative Super request: “From these approved notes, draft a handoff for revision 3. Separate delivered files, unresolved questions and recorded decisions. Preserve source references. Do not infer approval from filenames or send the message. Return a draft for me to review.”
If the client requests a change after approval, produce a new revision and ask which decisions must be revisited. An assistant can help organize the question; the agreed review process decides the answer. No automated sign-off is demonstrated here.


When the files are coming the other way
For incoming assets, Dropbox file requests let people upload without a Dropbox account. Uploads consume your storage, and file-size limits depend on the recipient plan. A collection mechanism can remove the need to invite every contributor into a working folder. It still does not establish that an uploaded asset is licensed or approved for your campaign.
Request the asset's intended use and relevant permission record through your agreed process. Keep confidential material in authorized systems, and do not paste it into an assistant merely to produce a more detailed note.


What Super's cache can and cannot reuse
The public Computer-Use Cache is inspectable software, not just a marketing label. Its pinned implementation reuses eligible responses for matching normalized requests; misses go upstream. Expiry and bypass controls exist. Streaming misses are not stored on that path.
A hit avoids another upstream model call, not all infrastructure costs. It does not prove a file still exists, permissions are unchanged or the client approved a new revision. Fresh external state needs fresh verification. The code is not a benchmark against Dropbox or evidence of every production deployment's configuration.


Price the missing capability
Dropbox's current plans list Basic with 2 GB free storage and paid tiers with different storage, recovery and sharing features. The page has monthly/yearly billing controls; confirm the selected cycle and current regional checkout rather than treating an extracted dollar figure as a quote. Team plans also have seat requirements.
Super advertises pay-as-you-go wallet usage. There is no verified per-handoff cost here. Trial one bounded preparation task and inspect its usage. Do not buy an assistant to replace storage capacity, or a larger storage plan to solve an unclear review process.


A better first test
Use one non-sensitive deliverable. Can the recipient identify the revision, open it, understand the requested decision and find the approval reference later? That is a more useful success criterion than how quickly a tool produces a confident-sounding summary.
