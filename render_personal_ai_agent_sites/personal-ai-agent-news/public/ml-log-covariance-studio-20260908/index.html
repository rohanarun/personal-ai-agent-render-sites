Two routes between covariances

Both endpoints have determinant one. Their arithmetic average can grow in area. Their log-Euclidean interpolation keeps that area unchanged.

A = diag(a, 1/a)
B = R(phi) A R(phi)T
Both are symmetric positive definite.

Average in a different space

M(t) = (1 - t)A + tB
L(t) = exp((1 - t)log(A) + t log(B))

For a symmetric positive-definite matrix C = Q diag(lambda) QT, its principal matrix logarithm is Q diag(log(lambda)) QT. The exponential reverses this operation. This is not an entrywise logarithm: zero or negative off-diagonal entries do not invalidate a positive-definite matrix.

The log-Euclidean framework performs linear operations after the matrix-log transform. Both paths here remain positive definite. Preserving determinant is a geometric choice, not a guarantee of better predictions or a universally superior covariance estimator.

Why the area changes

det M(t) = 1 + t(1 - t)(a - 1/a)2 sin2(phi)
det L(t) = exp((1 - t) log det A + t log det B) = 1

These endpoints have equal eigenvalues but different orientations. Arithmetic mixing spreads variance across directions, with maximum determinant at the midpoint. At a = 1 or phi = 0, the endpoints coincide and the paths agree. At phi = 90 degrees and t = 0.5, L is the identity while M is ((a + 1/a)/2) times the identity.

Ellipses are square-root images of the unit circle, with area pi sqrt(det C). They are not labeled probability-mass contours.
