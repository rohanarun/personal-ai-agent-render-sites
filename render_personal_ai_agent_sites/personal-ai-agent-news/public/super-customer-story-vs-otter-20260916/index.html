Build a chain from claim to source

Suppose a fictional interviewee says, “We stopped copying the weekly totals by hand.” A draft changes that to “We cut reporting time in half.” The second sentence introduces a measurement the first does not contain. A fluent rewrite can therefore be less defensible than an awkward original.

  1. Confirm the allowed use. Establish recording and sharing expectations through your organization's process before supplying material to any tool. Do not assume participation in an interview authorizes a public testimonial.
  2. Preserve the source. Keep the recording or approved transcript reference, speaker, timestamp range and version. Check uncertain names, numbers and negations against the recording.
  3. Separate wording types. Store exact excerpts separately from paraphrases. A paraphrase should not acquire quotation marks simply because it sounds natural.
  4. Challenge outcome claims. For a percentage, ask for baseline, time period, measurement method and attribution. If the evidence is absent, remove the number rather than estimate it.
  5. Request scoped review. Send the precise wording, named attribution, intended channel and relevant context for approval. Keep the response alongside that version; a later rewrite needs another review.

This is an editorial workflow, not legal advice or a claim that any product enforces your approval policy.

A bounded request for Super
Using only these approved materials, create a customer-story outline. Keep exact transcript excerpts separate from proposed paraphrases. Attach source locations to factual claims. Flag missing measurements and contradictions. Draft a review request that identifies wording, attribution and publication channel. Do not contact the customer or publish anything.
Include the story objective, the approved source set, a list of facts that must not be disclosed, and the person responsible for review. Inspect the result for invented causality: “we adopted the product, then revenue increased” does not establish that the product caused the increase.
Transfer the reviewed brief manually into your editorial process. This page offers no automatic Otter import, customer outreach, approval collection or publishing. You should evaluate those separate steps before granting an agent access.
