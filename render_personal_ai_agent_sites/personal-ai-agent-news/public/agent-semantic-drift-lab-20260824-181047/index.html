Fluency can hide a moving target.

Semantic drift happens when a word, label, or instruction keeps its shape while its context changes. An agent may appear consistent because it recognizes the phrase, even as the user’s goal, tool state, or domain convention has moved underneath it. The safest response is often to ground the phrase in a current example.

This lab makes the tradeoff visible. Grounding quality creates room to act. Shift, ambiguity, and sensitivity consume it. The score is a teaching device, not a language metric; use it to decide which question would turn a fluent guess into a shared meaning.
