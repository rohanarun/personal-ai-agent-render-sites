Observe a direction

The unknown vector (U, V) is jointly Gaussian with mean zero, unit marginal variances, and the chosen correlation. The measurement is W = h-transpose (U, V) + noise, with h = (cos(angle), sin(angle)). Noise is independent, zero-mean Gaussian with the selected variance. The observed scalar is w.

Writing the prior covariance as S, the measurement variance is d = h-transpose S h + noise variance. The conditional mean is S h w / d. The conditional covariance is S - (S h)(S h)-transpose / d. This is the Gaussian block-conditioning formula applied to the joint vector (U, V, W).

Value moves; noise reshapes

With the model fixed, changing w moves the conditional mean but does not change conditional covariance. Increasing independent noise weakens the measurement and leaves more uncertainty. Prior correlation determines whether observing one coordinate also informs the other.

The covariance reduction is positive semidefinite: variance cannot increase along any direction under this fixed Gaussian model. That does not mean the translated pink contour must sit inside the prior contour. A surprising measurement can move its center far away.

Exact observations are different

At zero noise variance, the measurement is an exact linear constraint. Conditional variance along h is zero, so the two-dimensional contour collapses to a segment on the constraint line. The remaining uncertainty is one-dimensional; there is no ordinary density over the full plane. The rank display uses a 1e-9 eigenvalue tolerance.

The contours visualize covariance geometry, not observed samples or estimated confidence intervals. A two-standard-deviation ellipse in two dimensions is not the familiar one-dimensional 95% interval. This is a known-parameter Gaussian model, not a fitted model, causal intervention, or guarantee about real sensor errors. Non-Gaussian, dependent, or misspecified noise can invalidate these conclusions.
