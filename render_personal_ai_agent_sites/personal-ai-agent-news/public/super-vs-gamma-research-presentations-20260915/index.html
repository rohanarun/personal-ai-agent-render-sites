Prepare a claim ledger before a slide outline

Start with the audience's decision: approve a pilot, choose a vendor, or investigate a result. Collect only the evidence needed for that decision. For each intended slide, record the proposed claim, source, observation date, units, population, calculation and limitation.

That structure catches errors that formatting cannot fix. “Revenue grew” needs a period, currency, and accounting definition. “Customers prefer this” needs a population and a method. “The redesign caused the lift” needs evidence for causality, not just a before-and-after table.

  1. Reconcile the inputs. Check whether two figures use the same period, definition and population. Do not silently merge incompatible values.
  2. Keep transformations visible. Preserve numerator and denominator for rates. Distinguish percentage points from relative percentage changes.
  3. Separate evidence from recommendation. A proposed next step is a judgment. Label it and show what would change the recommendation.
  4. Carry sources into the final artifact. A source list in a research chat does not automatically survive a slide export. Inspect the exported deck and its appendix.

A useful Super pilot brief

Prepare an evidence-led outline for the stated decision using only the approved sources. Keep each claim with its source reference, date, units and limitations. Show calculations from the original values. Flag conflicting definitions and missing evidence. Do not turn association into causation. Return an internal outline and unresolved questions; do not upload, publish or send it.

Supply a small, non-sensitive source set first. Check the result against the originals. If a source is inaccessible, that should appear as a gap rather than an invented citation. If a calculation is derived, it should be reproducible from the supplied values.

The proposed output is a research artifact. Moving it into Gamma is a separate step, whether done manually or through an approved integration. This article has not verified that handoff. Gamma's documented API means browser automation is not automatically the best implementation route.

Review the deck after export

Inspect the actual format the audience will receive. Check text overflow, chart labels, source notes, links and whether the caveats remained with the claims. Confirm that decorative graphics are not mistaken for measured data. An editable source project is not proof that its PowerPoint or PDF export preserved the intended meaning.

What the computer-use cache adds, precisely

The public computer-use-cache repository provides inspectable implementation, not just a product slogan. Its OpenAI-compatible proxy can store eligible successful JSON responses and reuse them for matching canonical requests. Misses are forwarded upstream; expiry and bypass controls are documented.

The reviewed source includes model, messages, tools and most request-body fields in the matching input. A hit can avoid an upstream model call. It does not refresh a source, verify a calculation, render an exported deck or prove completion.

For research work, a revised input must remain a revised input. Do not drop changed figures or source content to obtain a cache hit. We have not measured end-to-end savings on this presentation workflow, and the existence of the cache does not establish an automatic Gamma integration.

Our recommendation

Choose Gamma when your bottleneck is slide authoring and delivery. Evaluate Super Powers AI when a specific research gap needs investigation across approved sources. Keep evidence review and final-format inspection in either workflow. The deck should make the argument easier to understand, not stronger than the evidence allows.
