The gap below equality

The Lorenz curve starts at (0,0), accumulates sorted loads, and ends at (1,1) when total load is positive. This demo reports twice the area between the diagonal and that curve: G = 1 - 2 times the trapezoidal area under the curve.

Why the maximum is 0.875

For eight equally weighted experts, placing all tokens on one expert gives G = 7/8. We do not multiply by 8/7 to force a maximum of one. Equal positive loads give zero. All-zero loads have no token-share distribution, so the score is undefined.

A descriptive metric, not a router loss

This is an illustrative load-concentration diagnostic, not the auxiliary training objective used by Switch Transformers. It does not model expert capacity, dropped tokens, communication, specialization, or answer quality. Equal counts alone cannot establish a good router.

What the score forgets

Sorting discards expert identity. Permuting loads leaves G unchanged; multiplying every load by a positive constant also leaves it unchanged. The total remains visible because equally concentrated batches can contain very different amounts of work.
