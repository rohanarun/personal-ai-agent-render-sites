Minimizing s
Chernoff information, nats

A finite bound, an asymptotic rate

Z(s) = p^s q^(1-s) + (1-p)^s (1-q)^(1-s)
Error(n) <= 0.5 Z(s)^n, for 0 <= s <= 1
C = -log(min Z(s))

For equal priors, choosing the more likely hypothesis minimizes error. Summing the smaller binomial mass and dividing by two gives that exact error. Because min(a,b) is at most a^s b^(1-s), summing over independent sequences gives the displayed bound.

Minimizing Z tightens the bound. The Chernoff information C is the asymptotic error exponent, not the exact finite-n error: for distinct coins, -log(Error(n))/n approaches C. Natural logarithms define C here.

At s=0 or 1 the bound is 1/2. Identical coins cannot be distinguished and have error 1/2 for every n. For distinct coins, the minimizing weight solves Z'(s)=0; the implementation uses its binary closed form. jStat evaluates binomial masses. Independence and known coin probabilities are assumptions, not inferred properties of data.
