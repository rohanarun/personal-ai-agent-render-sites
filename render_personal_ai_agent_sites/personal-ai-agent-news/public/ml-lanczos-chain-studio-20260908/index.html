A short recurrence, a growing subspace
Start with a unit vector q. Each step multiplies it by A, removes the previous direction and its own component, then normalizes the remaining vector. The alpha values form T's diagonal; beta values join neighboring directions.
The relation is AQ = QT + r emT. In exact arithmetic, Q is orthonormal and T = QTAQ. Its eigenvalues are Ritz values, not automatically exact eigenvalues of A.
A zero residual means the reached subspace is invariant: no new direction exists. Starting in only one eigendirection ends immediately. Repeated eigenvalues can also shorten the chain; a single starting vector cannot recover every direction in a repeated eigenspace.
This four-dimensional experiment uses ml-matrix products and eigensolvers, with two reorthogonalization passes to control roundoff. It stops below residual norm 10-10, reports numerical errors, and is not an implicitly restarted large-scale solver.
