A concrete consultant handoff

Suppose your private note says a client is considering a new internal workshop. It includes names, a budget and candid concerns. The outside research need is much narrower: “What do the public accessibility pages of these three venues say about step-free entry?” The private context does not need to leave the notebook.

  1. Write the external question separately. Use public venue names and official pages. Remove the client identity, internal budget and private commentary. Redaction must preserve enough context for the question to make sense.
  2. Specify evidence, not a confident recommendation. Request source URL, access date, the relevant claim and anything not stated. “Not mentioned on the page” does not mean “not available.”
  3. Limit the action. Research only. No bookings, outreach, account access or promises to the client. If a page cannot be read, report the gap rather than searching private accounts.
  4. Verify the return. Open each cited official page. Separate exact observations from interpretation. For important access requirements, direct confirmation with the venue may still be necessary; a web page is not an on-site inspection.
  5. Bring back a small note. Record sources, checked date, unresolved questions and your conclusion. Link it to the private project manually. Do not automatically overwrite the original note or treat an old result as current availability.

The prerequisites are simple: a public question, permission to share the chosen inputs and time to check the evidence. This article has not run that venue task and makes no claim about any particular venue.

Reusable work is not private storage
Super's computer-use-cache has public implementation evidence, not just a marketing label. The pinned server normalizes requests into cache keys and stores eligible requests and responses. Hits can avoid an upstream model call; misses fall back upstream. TTL and bypass controls govern reuse, and streaming misses are not stored in this implementation.
Do not confuse that cache with an encrypted personal notebook. Avoid including private notes in a public research request. A cache hit also does not establish that a source was checked today: require fresh evidence when freshness matters. Inspect storage, retention and access controls before deploying a cache around sensitive data. This source review does not audit every production configuration or prove a task's total cost is zero.


When not to add an agent
You already know the source
If the answer is one click away, read it and keep the note. Delegation can add verification overhead rather than remove it.
The question depends on private context
Keep the work local unless you have an approved sharing arrangement. Deleting names is not sufficient when the remaining details identify a client or reveal confidential plans.
You need a reliable knowledge base
Choose Obsidian for the notebook requirement. An agent output is an artifact to evaluate and file, not a substitute for ownership, organization or backups.
For a trial, compare one manually completed public research ticket with one delegated ticket of similar scope. Record usable claims, unsupported claims, source freshness and review time. That is more useful than counting generated words. Stop if the supervision cost outweighs the help.
