Super Powers AI vs Sessionize: research a program fit without turning a public profile into an assumed booking.

September 15, 2026 · Published by Super Powers AI · Affiliated comparison

The shortlist is not the booking

Suppose you are organizing a technical meetup for engineers who have deployed their first AI feature. You want a practical talk about evaluating failures, not a broad introduction. A profile that says “AI speaker” is a lead. A past talk abstract with a relevant worked example is more useful evidence. Neither tells you whether the person is available on your date.

This is an illustrative program brief, not research about any real individual. A useful shortlist records the public material supporting topic fit, what remains unverified and the next respectful question. Do not infer a person's fees, availability, accessibility requirements or willingness to travel from their biography.

Start with Sessionize if you need speaker discovery and an organized call-for-speakers workflow. Try a bounded Super task if you already have permitted source material and need a research memo before making human-reviewed contact. No comparative benchmark or successful booking is reported here.

Different roles in the same process

Sessionize: profiles through program management

Sessionize offers public speaker profiles and a searchable directory. Speakers can maintain biographies, topics and talks; organizers can collect submissions, evaluate them, communicate with speakers and build a schedule. This is broader than a directory of names. Speaker features and organizer features.

Use the structured submission process when you need consistent information from candidates. Keep the operational status there rather than treating a separate research note as an acceptance or invitation.

Super: a source-linked preparation pilot

Super's public product describes automated research, browser and cloud-sandbox work, with developer access through MCP and a desktop CLI. Those capabilities provide a basis for testing a memo from organizer-supplied public profiles, talk pages and permitted recordings. Super product information.

Ask for the specific evidence linking a talk to your audience and a list of questions, not an unsupported “best speakers” ranking. The proposed task does not establish a native Sessionize integration, permission to scrape profiles at scale or a completed booking workflow.

If Sessionize's existing profiles and evaluation tools already meet your needs, keep them. Adding another assistant should solve a defined preparation problem rather than duplicate the same records.

What can this source actually establish?

Three fictional research situations. These examples help structure a review; they are not judgments about real speakers.

A profile lists the right topic

Supported: the speaker publicly describes that topic as an area of interest or expertise.

Still missing: whether a proposed talk has the depth, prerequisites and format your audience needs. Locate a relevant abstract or ask for an outline rather than inferring it from a keyword.

Next question: “Could you share a proposed outline aimed at engineers who have shipped an initial AI feature?”

A recording looks relevant

Supported: the specific content you reviewed appears in that recording. Record the title, URL and timestamp for the relevant example.

Still missing: whether the talk is current, can be adapted to your time slot, or may be recorded at your event. A public recording does not establish permission to reuse slides or video.

Next question: “Would this material fit a 30-minute practical session, and what recording arrangements would you prefer?”

The person spoke nearby last year

Supported: a dated event page lists a past appearance.

Still missing: current location, travel willingness, date availability and terms. Do not treat prior attendance as a current commitment or estimate private details.

Next question: “Would you be interested and available for this date and format? We can share the event details and discuss arrangements.”

A small research workflow worth testing

  1. Define the audience first. Write the experience level, learning outcome, duration, format and event date. Keep logistical constraints separate from a judgment of topic fit.
  2. Bound the sources. Start with a few organizer-selected public profiles and talk pages. Respect site access rules; do not collect private contact details or unrelated personal information.
  3. Build an evidence note. Record the material reviewed, its date, the relevant passage or timestamp, and the connection to the program brief. Label suggestions as suggestions and unanswered questions as unknown.
  4. Review before outreach. An organizer verifies the note, checks the appropriate contact route and approves any message. Do not bulk-send invitations or submit forms automatically.
  5. Keep replies authoritative. Update availability, terms and consent from the person's actual response. If you use Sessionize, retain acceptance and scheduling state there. A research draft does not change it.

Evaluate a pilot by useful evidence notes, unsupported claims, correction time and actual spend. Do not measure success by the number of profiles collected. This article has not executed the pilot or measured either product on it.

What the cache reuses

Super publishes an OpenAI-compatible cache proxy that reuses eligible successful responses for matching canonical requests and forwards misses upstream. Its pinned implementation is inspectable technology, not evidence of an invitation or a speaker's consent.

Retain source-specific and event-specific context in the actual request. A new date or profile identifier only in metadata does not refresh the default cache key because metadata is excluded. Update the source content or bypass caching when fresh information is needed.

A cache hit avoids another upstream model call; it does not recheck availability, authorize contact or remove all browser and infrastructure costs. Never reuse one person's inferred preferences as another person's facts.
