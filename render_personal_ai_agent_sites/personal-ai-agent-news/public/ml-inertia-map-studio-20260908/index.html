Signs survive a bijection

Sylvester’s law states that a real symmetric A and B = SᵀAS have the same counts of positive, negative, and zero eigenvalues when S is invertible. Here S = [[a, b], [0, 1]], so det(S) = a. The eigenvalue magnitudes generally change; their signs, counted with multiplicity, do not.

Why? Every subspace on which xᵀAx is positive maps bijectively to one of equal dimension under x = Sy. The same argument applies to negative directions. Rank preservation fixes the remaining zero count.

The singular exception

At a = 0, S loses a coordinate. The map is no longer bijective, and the theorem no longer applies. For the saddle, B then has eigenvalues 0 and 2b² − 1: the negative direction can disappear completely. That is loss of information, not a better-conditioned version of the same problem.

A Hessian connection

Under a fixed invertible linear reparameterization x = Sy, a loss Hessian transforms by congruence. Its positive and negative curvature counts are preserved, even though step sizes and conditioning may change. This differs from similarity S⁻¹AS, which preserves eigenvalues themselves. The experiment is a quadratic model, not a claim about global neural-network loss landscapes.
