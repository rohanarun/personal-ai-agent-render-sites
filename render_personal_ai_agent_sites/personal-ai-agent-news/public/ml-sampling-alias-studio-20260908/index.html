Same evidence, different signals

The signal is cos(2 pi f t), with unit amplitude and zero phase. At sample times t = n / fs, adding an integer multiple of fs to f leaves every sample unchanged. Cosine symmetry also folds frequencies above fs / 2 back into the baseband.

The folded frequency is min(r, fs - r), where r is f modulo fs. At 12 Hz sampled at 16 Hz, the samples match a 4 Hz cosine. This is a constructed example, not a learned reconstruction.

Before time-series learning

Sampling must be paired with appropriate bandwidth assumptions and anti-alias filtering before digitization or downsampling. Otherwise a time-series model may see a false low-frequency pattern. These controls show ideal noiseless sampling, not a physical filter.

The Nyquist boundary needs care: a zero-phase cosine remains visible there, while a sine at that frequency can vanish at all sample times. Do not treat exactly twice the frequency as a universal guarantee for arbitrary phase.

FFT conventions

The FFT uses 64 samples at n / fs for n = 0 through 63. Amplitude is 2 times the complex magnitude divided by 64, except DC and Nyquist, which are not doubled. Integer signal frequencies and the offered rates place these examples on FFT bins, avoiding leakage. The waveform panel shows only the first second; the spectrum uses the complete record.
