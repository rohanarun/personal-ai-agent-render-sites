Where a determinant comes from

Six feature vectors, fifteen pairs. A pair that points in two different directions spans area. Its squared area contributes to the determinant of the full feature Gram matrix.

From local area to global spread

Each row of X is one two-dimensional feature vector. For rows i and j, the signed determinant is x[i] y[j] - y[i] x[j]. Reversing their order flips its sign; squaring removes orientation. The Cauchy-Binet identity says that det(X transpose X) is the sum of these squared minors.

When the sum is positive, dividing each squared area by the sum gives a distribution over pairs. Parallel and zero vectors contribute zero together. A common rotation preserves every pair area. Scaling every vector by c multiplies each squared area, and the total, by c to the fourth power.

A feature-design lens

In a linear model with two coefficients, X transpose X is the unregularized normal matrix. All rows on one line leave a coefficient direction unidentified. This raw, uncentered matrix is not a covariance estimate. A larger determinant is sensitive to feature units and is not, by itself, evidence of predictive quality.

The examples here are synthetic, with no fitted model or sampling claim. Pair shares are withheld when the total is numerically zero; they must not be interpreted as a uniform distribution in that case.

Keith Ball's combinatorics notes, Cauchy-Binet theorem (PDF page 224) establish the squared-minor identity. The feature arrangements and pair-share exploration are original worked examples.
