Three processes, three lessons

Periodic does not mean unstable arithmetic

The pure cycle sends A to B, B to C, and C to A with probability one. Starting at A repeats every three steps. Starting uniformly stays uniform, even though individual paths keep moving. A stationary distribution can exist without every initial distribution converging to it.

A self-loop changes the dynamics

The lazy cycle assigns equal weight to staying and moving forward. Its distribution approaches uniform from any initial state. The absorbing preset lets probability move toward C; once there, it stays. These statements describe the presets, not arbitrary edited matrices.

Probability is not a sampled path

This is exact distribution propagation up to floating-point rounding, not a Monte Carlo sample. Each step computes p(next) = p(now) P. Entry P[i,j] is the probability of moving from i to j. Each row must sum to one. Last-step change is half the sum of absolute differences between consecutive distributions; a small value alone does not prove a general convergence theorem.

A fixed transition matrix assumes the next-state distribution depends only on the current state and does not change with time. Editing weights or the starting distribution restarts the history.
