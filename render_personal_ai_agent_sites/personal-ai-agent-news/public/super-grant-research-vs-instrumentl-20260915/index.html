A promising match is a research task

A community arts nonprofit finds an opportunity that mentions youth education. The mission sounds right. But the linked page is from a previous cycle, the current service-area requirement is unclear, and the team does not know whether an invitation is required. Drafting a proposal now would hide the actual bottleneck: missing evidence.

This is a fictional example, not an open funding opportunity. The useful output is a short review memo: the official opportunity page, current-cycle evidence, unresolved requirements and a named person responsible for the next check. Neither an AI summary nor a matching score establishes eligibility or promises an award.

Three questions before drafting

These editorial checks organize research. They are not an eligibility determination; consult the current funder materials and the responsible program contact when unclear.

Is this the current application cycle?

A search result may point to last year's announcement. Record the cycle identifier, the official page or document version, and the date you retrieved it. If a current cycle is not established, mark it unknown rather than copying the old deadline forward.

Memo wording: “Prior-cycle page located. Current cycle and deadline require confirmation.”

Does the published requirement match our facts?

Separate the funder's stated conditions from your organization's documented facts. For the fictional arts program, “serves young people” does not resolve a geographic restriction or invitation requirement. Preserve the relevant source passage and ask the person who can verify the organization's situation.

Memo wording: “Mission appears relevant; service-area and invitation requirements remain unresolved.”

Can the team make an informed next decision?

List missing inputs, their owners and an internal review date. Distinguish the funder's deadline from the team's earlier decision date. A completed memo may still recommend seeking clarification or stopping research; it need not recommend applying.

Memo wording: “Research only. Program lead to resolve open questions before proposal effort is approved.”

Run a small, reviewable pilot

  1. Bound the source list. Start with three public opportunities, not a demand to find every possible grant. Supply a non-sensitive program description and a reviewer-approved set of facts. Do not upload donor records or credentials.
  2. Ask for separate evidence fields. Opportunity and cycle, official URL, retrieval date, stated requirements, organization facts, unresolved questions and next owner. Keep missing information explicit.
  3. Review the original material. The human reviewer opens the linked sources and checks every date and requirement used in the memo. A page mentioning a theme is not enough to support a conclusion about a specific program.
  4. Record the decision in one place. Keep the authoritative pipeline in the tool your team already uses. Do not automatically submit, contact a funder or change an application's status from a generated memo.

Compare accepted source-backed statements, missed questions, correction time and actual task spend. A longer shortlist is not necessarily better. This article has not run that pilot and does not report saved hours, coverage or awards.

The cache does not make old facts current

Super publishes an OpenAI-compatible cache proxy. Its documented mechanism stores eligible successful responses and reuses them for matching canonical requests; misses go upstream. The pinned implementation exposes that mechanism rather than requiring you to trust a broad marketing claim.

For changing opportunity pages, include the freshly retrieved content in the actual request or bypass caching. Adding a retrieval date only to metadata is not enough: metadata is excluded from the default cache key. A hit avoids another upstream model call, but does not re-open the source, prove a deadline, submit an application or eliminate every execution cost.

Reusable reasoning and a current factual record are different things. Never remove cycle-specific differences merely to get a cache hit.
