Before you call it done

Notion vs Super Powers AI for client updates that separate completed work from accepted delivery.

A polished update can still make the wrong promise. “The new dashboard is done” may mean a developer merged code, a tester checked staging, or a client accepted the release. A useful reporting workflow names the milestone and its evidence instead of making those states sound interchangeable.

Start with the record, not the prose

For a small agency delivering a client dashboard, maintain one row per deliverable: agreed scope, accountable owner, evidence reference, environment, observation time, acceptance condition and next decision. A percentage-complete field cannot replace these. An old green status should remain a dated observation, not silently become a current guarantee.

Suppose Friday's code review says the export button was implemented. Monday's client update needs a different question: has someone verified the agreed export in the intended environment, and has the client accepted it if the contract requires acceptance? A merged change is useful evidence, but it is not the same evidence as a successful export or approval.

Ask for missing evidence precisely. “Please confirm the CSV matches the agreed columns using the staging build linked here” is actionable. “Any updates?” creates another conversation to summarize. Do not invent a due date or imply that a client caused a delay when the record only says a response is pending.

Check the actual cost boundary
Notion's pricing distinguishes limited AI trials on Free and Plus from the broader Business and Enterprise offering. Custom Agents use a separate credit model; the page lists $10 per 1,000 monthly Notion credits. Do not treat a credit as one report: work per run affects usage. Seat prices depend on currency and billing selection, so this article does not quote an ambiguous localized seat total. Verify your Notion plan and billing options.
Compare the total trial cost, review effort and maintenance burden, not just a subscription headline. No Super price advantage or time-saving percentage was measured here. If Notion already has the permitted evidence and produces an adequate draft, keeping one system may be the simpler choice.


What the computer-use cache proves
Super's creator publishes computer-use-cache. The pinned server implementation normalizes and hashes requests, stores eligible successful JSON responses, and can serve matching responses without another upstream model call. Expiry and bypass controls exist; a miss goes upstream.
That is inspectable technology, but cached text is not current delivery evidence. A response about last week's staging build cannot certify today's release. Version and evidence timestamps must survive the drafting process, and a fresh check may be necessary. The repository does not prove every production configuration, browser action or customer saving.


Run a small, reviewable trial
Use one sanitized project, one reporting period and the same evidence for both approaches. Keep confidential client material out unless permission and data policies allow processing. Require the draft to separate implemented, verified and accepted work, list blockers without guessing blame, and link each factual claim to its source.
Have the project owner mark corrections before anything is sent. Count unsupported completion claims and measure review time. Keep the better workflow only if it improves that real task. Approval to draft is not approval to send, alter the project record or promise a delivery date.
