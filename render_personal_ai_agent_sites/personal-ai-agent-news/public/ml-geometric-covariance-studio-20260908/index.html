A coordinate-consistent midpoint

For positive-definite A and B, the geometric mean G is the unique positive-definite solution of GA⁻¹G = B. It equals A½(A⁻½BA⁻½)½A½. These are matrix square roots, not square roots of individual entries.

A change of coordinates S replaces each covariance C with SCSᵀ. Computing the mean afterward gives SGSᵀ. The congruence residual compares those two routes. This property does not distinguish G from the arithmetic mean, which also transforms this way.

The geometric mean is no larger than M = (A+B)/2 in positive-semidefinite order. The displayed smallest eigenvalue of M-G checks that relation. Also, det(G) = √(det(A)det(B)); this is not an entrywise averaging rule.

Here A starts as diag(4,1), B has eigenvalues r and 1/r, and S = [[1,s],[0,1]]. ml-matrix evaluates square roots by symmetric eigendecomposition. The examples illustrate covariance geometry relevant to metric learning, not improved predictive accuracy.
