The weighting is the point

Rows of the transition matrix are normalized symmetric edge weights, including the self-loop once. Stationary probabilities are proportional to those row sums. Positive off-diagonal weights keep this finite chain irreducible.

Kemeny's theorem states that sum over targets j of stationary(j) m(i,j) is independent of start i. Uniform target weights do not generally have that property, although symmetric examples can make the two choices coincide.

For each target, the off-diagonal times solve h(i) = 1 + sum over k other than the target of P(i,k) h(k). The studio solves these systems with ml-matrix. A separate matrix identity gives K = trace((I - P + 1 stationary)^(-1)); the displayed discrepancy compares both computations.

This page uses the positive-time convention: even if the target equals the start, at least one transition is required. With immediate arrival allowed, the diagonal becomes zero and the stationary-weighted constant is smaller by exactly one. This is an expected hitting-time identity, not a mixing-time guarantee or a simulated sample average.
