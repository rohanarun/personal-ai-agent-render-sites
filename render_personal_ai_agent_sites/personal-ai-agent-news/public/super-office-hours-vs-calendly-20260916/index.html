A repeatable mentor workflow

  1. Set the boundary. Pick the session duration, audience, meeting location and topics you can actually help with. Add breathing room between calls in the scheduling system.
  2. Ask one useful intake question. “What decision do you want to leave with?” is more actionable than a request for an unrestricted life story. Avoid collecting unnecessary sensitive details.
  3. Prepare from approved material. For a portfolio review, a public portfolio link and the mentee's stated question may be enough. A proposed Super task is to draft three observations with links, keeping uncertain interpretations separate.
  4. Review the brief yourself. Confirm that the cited pages belong to the right person and are current. Do not turn inferred experience into a fact.
  5. End with a small commitment. Record one next step, its owner and a proposed date. Ask the participant to confirm; a generated recap is not agreement.

Illustrative example: a designer asks which of two case studies to lead with. Prepare evidence about those two projects, not an unsolicited career assessment. During the call, test the reasoning together. A good brief leaves room for the participant to correct your assumptions.

Leave room for the next step

This local planning example reserves three minutes to frame the question and four to agree on a next step. The rest is discussion time. It neither books a meeting nor checks anyone's calendar.

Equal topic allocation is an illustrative planning rule, not an optimized meeting strategy. A topic needing more context may need a separate session.

Choose the smallest complete workflow
Use Calendly alone when booking, its available meeting tools and a short manual template cover the job. Trial Super alongside it when the missing work is approved research across sources, not calendar mechanics. Start draft-only and have a human approve anything sent to the participant.
Do not record or transcribe participants just because a tool offers it; follow your organization's consent and data policies. Neither service should be assumed to be approved for confidential mentoring material without checking.
