Beyond the eigenvalue dots

The numerical range is W(A) = {z*Az : ||z|| = 1}, where * means conjugate transpose. It is convex and contains every eigenvalue. Normal matrices reduce it to the convex hull of their eigenvalues.

Here A = [[-1, c], [0, 1]] and z = [cos(theta), exp(i phi) sin(theta)]. The eigenvalues remain -1 and 1. The ellipse has semiaxes sqrt(1 + c squared / 4) and c / 2; at c = 0 it collapses to the real segment [-1, 1].

The rightmost value is the largest eigenvalue of (A + A*)/2. Its eigenvector supplies the exact boundary witness. The numerical radius is the greatest modulus in W(A), not the largest eigenvalue modulus; it lies between half the matrix 2-norm and the full 2-norm.

Samples use uniform complex unit-vector directions, not uniform area inside the ellipse. Each coupling change clears samples from the previous matrix.
