Recipes, then groceries

How to build a shopping plan without guessing quantities: Paprika vs a proposed Super Powers AI workflow.

Three saved recipes are not yet a grocery list. Each may use a different serving count, a different form of the same ingredient, or something already in your kitchen. The useful automation is the one that preserves those details rather than confidently smoothing them away.

Resolve three questions before shopping

First, choose the actual recipe and intended number of servings. A recipe that makes four portions does not automatically match a household of four: you may want leftovers or be cooking only part of the meal. Keep the recipe's original yield and your intended yield visible, so any scaling can be checked.

Second, inspect ingredient forms. “Tomatoes,” “tomato paste” and “canned chopped tomatoes” are not interchangeable shopping lines simply because they share a word. Keep quantities and units attached. A count, a package and a weight are different descriptions; do not invent a conversion without the required information.

Third, check what you actually have. A pantry entry is a record, not a live sensor. If a housemate used an ingredient yesterday, the saved list may be stale. Record the remaining amount before removing it from the shopping plan. When uncertain, mark it for checking instead of presenting an exact purchase quantity.

A concrete example

Imagine two fictional recipes: one requires two cans of chopped tomatoes, and the other needs tomato paste. A sensible shopping draft keeps those separate. If one can is already available, subtract it only after confirming it is the required product and package size. Do not merge the paste into the tomato count or assume every can has the same amount.

That is a data-quality problem before it is an AI problem. A clean source recipe, checked servings and a current pantry record give any tool a better starting point. This article is about planning logistics, not nutrition, allergy screening or food-safety advice.

Record what is ready for review

This local checklist records your declarations only. It does not inspect recipes, count pantry stock or validate a diet. Even all three checks do not authorize a purchase.

Preparation checks


A cache is not a pantry sensor
Super's creator publishes the computer-use-cache repository. The pinned server implementation hashes normalized requests and can reuse eligible successful JSON responses. Misses go upstream; expiry and bypass controls affect reuse.
A matching response can avoid another upstream model call. It cannot establish current stock, today's store price or whether a purchase happened. Fresh household facts must be supplied or checked again. The repository is evidence of response-reuse technology, not a benchmark of this planning workflow or proof of every production configuration. Other execution costs can remain.


Test with a small shopping trip
Start with two recipes you already know. Compare the proposed list against the originals and your pantry. Count corrections for quantities, units and ingredient forms before judging convenience. Keep the owner in control of substitutions, retailer selection and spending. A drafted list is not an order, and an order confirmation is a separate result to verify.
