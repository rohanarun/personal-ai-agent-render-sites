Two-Step Reachability Studio

One input acts horizontally. Between actions, the system rotates and contracts. Can two inputs reach your target within one unit of total input energy?

x(next) = A x + B u. A is 0.8 times a planar rotation, B = [1, 0]^T, and the initial state is zero. Budget: u0 squared + u1 squared ≤ 1.

Green region: endpoints reachable within the energy budget. Burgundy cross: target. Dark circle: least-squares endpoint. The diagram shows final states, not the path through time.

Dynamics supply the missing direction

Starting from zero, x1 = B u0 and x2 = A B u0 + B u1. Thus the endpoint is a linear map of the two inputs. When B and A B are independent, every target is reachable in two steps with unrestricted inputs, despite having only one input at each step.

At zero or 180 degrees those directions align. The reachable set collapses to a horizontal segment under the energy budget, and targets with nonzero vertical coordinates become unreachable even with unlimited energy. This is a rank failure, not just an expensive control problem.

Unrestricted reach is not budgeted reach

The unit disk of input pairs maps to the green ellipse. Its semiaxis lengths are the singular values of [A B, B]. Near an aligned angle, the narrow direction requires large inputs. Full rank guarantees unrestricted reachability, not low cost.

The SVD solver returns the minimum-norm least-squares input pair. If its endpoint misses the target, the target is outside the reachable subspace. Otherwise, its squared norm is the minimum energy required. A value above one violates this example's budget. For an unreachable target, the shown energy belongs to the projected endpoint, not to reaching the target.

This is a discrete two-step linear example, not a physical robot controller. No per-action amplitude bound is imposed; the constraint is on total squared input. Rank and residual checks use 10^-10 and 10^-8 tolerances. The inputs and state sequence remain visible even when the proposed solution exceeds the energy budget.
