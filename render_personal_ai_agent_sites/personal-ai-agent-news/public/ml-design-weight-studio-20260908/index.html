A certificate, not a guess

The model is y = intercept + slope x + noise, with constant independent noise variance and x in [-1,1]. Relative weights are normalized to total mass one. The information matrix is M = sum w(x) f(x) f(x)^T, with f(x) = [1,x]. Doubling all weights changes nothing here: this experiment compares allocation, not sample size.

Writing mean = sum w(x)x and second moment = sum w(x)x^2 gives det(M) = second moment - mean^2. It cannot exceed one on this interval. Equal masses at -1 and +1 attain one, so they are D-optimal: they maximize the information determinant.

The Kiefer–Wolfowitz equivalence theorem links this to G-optimality. For normalized nonsingular linear-regression designs, the maximum sensitivity is at least the parameter count, two. Achieving two certifies optimality. Here sensitivity is a convex quadratic, so its interval maximum is attained at an endpoint; the certificate uses those endpoints, not a coarse plotted grid.

With all positive mass at one location, the intercept and slope cannot both be identified. The studio reports that singularity instead of inventing an inverse. Zero total weight is a missing design. The conclusion depends on the straight-line, equal-noise assumptions; it does not prescribe sampling for arbitrary ML models.
