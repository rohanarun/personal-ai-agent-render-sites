One observation, two possible distributions. A false-positive budget limits how often a test can raise an alarm. Randomization makes the best detection rate a continuous frontier.

Spend false alarms where they buy the most detection

maximize sum Q(x) g(x)
subject to sum P(x) g(x) <= budget
and 0 <= g(x) <= 1

The likelihood ratio Q(x)/P(x) is detection gained per unit of false-positive probability. Neyman-Pearson optimality prioritizes the largest ratios, with a randomized boundary decision when necessary. Equal ratios may admit different, equally good tests.

Gray points restrict every decision to zero or one. Allowing fractional decisions fills their convex hull; its upper edge is the ROC frontier. A straight segment corresponds to randomizing a decision. When P equals Q, detection equals the false-positive rate for every test.

This is a single-observation, simple-hypothesis experiment, not a fitted classifier or a guarantee about estimated real-world distributions. The LP solver computes the optimum. Your decisions remain independent of the budget until you choose the optimal test. Exports preserve unrounded values.
