Normalize at zero, control the interval

s(lambda) = (upper + 1 - 2 lambda) / (upper - 1)
p_n(lambda) = T_n(s(lambda)) / T_n(s(0))
bound = 1 / T_n(s(0))

The NIST Chebyshev identity, T_n(cos(theta)) = cos(n theta), implies absolute T_n is at most one on [-1,1]. Mapping the spectral interval there gives the displayed bound; dividing by T_n(s(0)) makes p_n(0) = 1.

For symmetric positive-definite A with spectrum in this interval, the Euclidean error satisfies ||p_n(A)e|| at most bound times ||e||. The comparison uses n Richardson steps with step size 2/(upper+1), whose interval bound is ((upper-1)/(upper+1)) to power n.

Here A = Q diag(1, (upper+1)/2, upper) Q transpose, with Q a fixed rotation of the first two coordinates by 30 degrees. The initial error is (1,1,1)/sqrt(3). Matrix recurrence and scalar spectral evaluation are computed separately and compared.

This illustrates error-polynomial design, not a full linear-system solver or a timing benchmark. The bound assumes a correct positive spectral interval and symmetry. Degree zero is the identity filter. Polynomial recurrence follows T_0 = 1, T_1 = x, T_(n+1) = 2x T_n - T_(n-1). Export contains the matrix, vectors, response samples and all degree results.
