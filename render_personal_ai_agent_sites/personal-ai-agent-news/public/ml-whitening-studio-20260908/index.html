Rotate, scale, optionally rotate back

Whitening centers the data and rescales its covariance directions. PCA whitening expresses the result in principal-component axes. ZCA rotates those scaled components back toward the original axes. Both produce identity sample covariance when the covariance is full rank and regularization is zero, up to numerical precision.

For centered row data X and covariance C = V diag(lambda) V^T, this studio uses X V diag(g) for PCA and X V diag(g) V^T for ZCA. The gain in each retained direction is g = 1/sqrt(lambda + epsilon). Eigenvectors and eigenvalues come from ml-matrix; PCA eigenvector signs are arbitrary.

Stability has a measurable cost

Small eigenvalues imply large unregularized gains. Adding epsilon reduces amplification, but the variance along each principal direction becomes lambda/(lambda+epsilon), not 1. The output covariance table exposes that departure. Regularization has the same units as variance.

A rank-deficient sample cannot supply two independent variance directions. Eigenvalues below 1e-9 times max(1, largest eigenvalue) are treated as zero, with gain zero rather than infinity. This pseudowhitening convention only normalizes the supported subspace. Coincident points remain zero.

Uncorrelated is not independent

Whitening removes second-order linear dependence in its fitted sample; it does not generally establish statistical independence. It can change distances and amplify directions dominated by noise. PCA and ZCA are two choices among many rotations, not competing claims about the true features.

For a learning pipeline, estimate the mean and transform using training data only, then apply that same transform to validation and test data. Re-fitting on held-out data leaks information. Identity covariance on these four synthetic points does not guarantee identity covariance, better accuracy, or fairness on future data.
