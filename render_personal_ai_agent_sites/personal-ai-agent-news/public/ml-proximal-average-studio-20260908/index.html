Averaging changes the objective

The average of proximal maps is itself a proximal map of a function called the proximal average. That function is generally not the arithmetic average of the original functions. Its minimizers instead match those of the sum of their Moreau envelopes.

Here each Hessian has eigenvalues at least 1, so each unit-parameter proximal map has Lipschitz constant at most 1/2. Their average is a contraction and converges to a unique fixed point. This guarantees convergence, not minimization of F.

For the displayed quadratics, the original optimum solves (A₁+A₂)x = A₁c₁+A₂c₂. Equal Hessians make the two limits agree. Different Hessians can move the averaged-prox limit and leave a positive original-loss penalty.
