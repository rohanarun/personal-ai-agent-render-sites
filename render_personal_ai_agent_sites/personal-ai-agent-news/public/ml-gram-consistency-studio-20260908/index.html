Similarities must fit together

If K contains inner products of real feature vectors, then v^T K v is the squared norm of their weighted sum. It cannot be negative. A symmetric matrix with nonnegative energy for every vector is positive semidefinite, or PSD. Equivalently, all its eigenvalues are nonnegative.

Each similarity here lies between -1 and 1, and every diagonal entry is one. Those pairwise bounds are necessary for unit-vector inner products, but they are not sufficient. In the contradiction preset, A resembles B and C strongly, while B and C strongly oppose each other.

A witness beats a reassuring example

The smallest eigenvalue is the minimum of v^T K v over unit vectors. Its eigenvector therefore supplies a negative-energy witness when the matrix is indefinite. A positive energy for one hand-picked vector proves nothing about all other directions. The zero vector always has zero energy, even for an invalid matrix.

The identical preset is singular but valid: PSD permits zero eigenvalues. Numerical classification uses tolerance 1e-9. Eigenvectors in a repeated-eigenvalue space are not unique, and a witness can flip sign without changing its energy.

A finite check is not a kernel proof

Kernel methods use similarities as inner products in a feature space. A kernel function must yield PSD Gram matrices for every finite choice of inputs. Passing this three-point test establishes only consistency of this one matrix, not validity of a function on an entire domain.

This studio performs a diagnostic, not a repair. It never silently clips negative eigenvalues or changes your similarities. A failed test suggests revisiting the similarity rule or its assumptions before using methods that require PSD structure.
