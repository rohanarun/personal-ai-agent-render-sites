Why the product is a valid kernel

For first-map vectors u and second-map vectors v, use the four features [u1 v1, u1 v2, u2 v1, u2 v2]. The dot product between two such rows factors into (u dot u-prime)(v dot v-prime). Thus the entrywise product of the two Gram matrices is itself a Gram matrix and is positive semidefinite.

For the average, concatenate [u1, u2, v1, v2] and divide by sqrt(2). Its Gram matrix is (A + B)/2. These maps combine interactions versus separate contributions. Neither option is trained or universally better.

Signed similarity is not a logical AND

Two negative similarities multiply to a positive value. A negative and a positive multiply to a negative value. The product therefore cannot be interpreted as requiring two nonnegative matches in this signed example. Positive semidefinite describes quadratic forms, not a requirement that every entry be positive.

Not ordinary matrix multiplication

Ordinary A times B sums across intervening items. The entrywise product multiplies only corresponding entries, with no sum. Even for two symmetric positive-semidefinite matrices, ordinary multiplication need not be symmetric.
