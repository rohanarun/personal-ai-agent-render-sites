Resistance sees every route

Regard each edge as an electrical conductor. Inject one unit of current at one node and remove it at another. Their voltage difference is effective resistance. Current can divide across alternate routes, so this is not shortest-path distance.

The weighted graph Laplacian has weighted degrees on its diagonal and negative edge weights off the diagonal. The calculation uses R(a,b) = (e_a - e_b)^T L+ (e_a - e_b), with the Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse supplied by ml-matrix. The displayed potentials use the zero-mean convention.

Which edges are redundant?

An edge's leverage is its conductance times the effective resistance between its endpoints. A bridge has leverage 1: no alternative route connects its two sides. In a connected four-node graph, edge leverage sums to 3. More generally it sums to the node count minus the number of components, including isolated nodes.

Resistance-based edge sampling underlies spectral graph sparsification: reducing edges while approximately preserving Laplacian quadratic forms. That structure matters in graph-based learning. A low score is not permission to delete every low-scoring edge at once. Proper sampling, reweighting, and error control are separate steps; this studio does not construct a guaranteed sparsifier.

Boundary cases are part of the answer

Across disconnected components, resistance is infinite and the unit-current experiment is infeasible. A node compared with itself has resistance zero. The export records disconnected resistance as null with an explicit status, never as a finite score. Weights are positive integers from 1 to 4 in this small undirected example.
