Separate two invariant directions by iterating toward a matrix whose square is the identity.

Signs of eigenvalues, not entries

For A = T diag(-a, b) inv(T), with T = [[1, s], [0, 1]] and a, b positive, sign(A) = T diag(-1, 1) inv(T). It squares to I. Entrywise signs would give a different operation.

Newton's inverse iteration converges to this matrix sign. Only at convergence do (I - X)/2 and (I + X)/2 become the negative- and positive-eigenvalue projectors. The colored components always sum to v; rings mark their exact targets. With a sheared basis these projections are oblique, not necessarily perpendicular.

In a continuous-time linear system, negative eigenvalues decay and positive ones grow. This two-dimensional example isolates those directions. Eigenvalues on the imaginary axis are excluded: the sign function is undefined there.
