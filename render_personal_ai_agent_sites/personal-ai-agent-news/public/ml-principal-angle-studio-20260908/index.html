Reference basis Q_A

The reference plane is the span of the first two coordinate axes. Both bases always have two orthonormal columns.

Cross-basis matrix

M = Q_A transpose Q_B

The same plane has many coordinate systems

A basis is a set of coordinates for a subspace, not the subspace itself. Multiplying Q_B on the right by a two-dimensional rotation mixes its columns but leaves its span and projector Q_B Q_B transpose unchanged. Directly pairing the first columns and then the second columns can therefore give a misleading comparison.

Principal angles remove that arbitrary pairing

For orthonormal bases, the singular values of Q_A transpose Q_B are the cosines of the principal angles. The angles lie between 0 and 90 degrees. Zero indicates a shared direction; two zero angles here mean identical planes. Two 90-degree angles mean the planes are orthogonal.

This studio sorts angles from smallest to largest, unlike SciPy's descending output. It combines singular values of the cross-basis matrix and the perpendicular residual to recover angles with atan2, avoiding reliance on arccos near 1. The JavaScript numerical engine is ml-matrix, not SciPy.

An independently checkable construction

Before basis rotation, the moving columns are [cos(a), 0, sin(a), 0] and [0, cos(b), 0, sin(b)]. Thus the two principal angles must be the two tilt settings in sorted order. The basis-rotation slider changes neither angle.

The displayed projector distance is the Frobenius norm of P_A - P_B. Expanding the squared norm gives 4 - 2 times the squared Frobenius norm of M, equivalently 2 times [sin(a) squared + sin(b) squared]. This identity provides another check that basis rotation changes coordinates without changing the subspace.

This synthetic construction can help interpret comparisons of learned feature spans, but it does not measure prediction quality, semantic equivalence, or causal similarity. All four coordinates are shown as numbers rather than a misleading three-dimensional projection. Small residuals reflect floating-point arithmetic.
