The useful weekly update is not a pile of links. It is a short account of what changed, what supports it and what your team should investigate next.

Three articles can describe one event

A competitor announces a pilot. A trade publication rewrites the release; an industry newsletter links to that story. A briefing that calls this “three independent confirmations of adoption” has added a conclusion that the source chain does not support.

For a product manager, this matters more than the length of the digest. One pilot may deserve a discovery question, not a roadmap change. Group the reports around the underlying event, retain their provenance, and separate the company's claim from your interpretation.

Our recommendation: choose Feedly Market Intelligence if your main need is sustained collection, team organization and distribution. Try Super on a bounded source-to-brief task if you already have the inputs and need to test a different handoff. Neither choice removes the need to review evidence.

This is a proposed workflow, not a demonstrated Feedly connector or a claim of complete market coverage.

Keep Feedly if its collection and newsletter workflow already does the job. Super should earn a role through the quality of a bounded pilot, not through a promise that a general browser assistant replaces every intelligence database or distribution system.

A briefing structure that stays honest

  1. Start with a decision question. Choose a narrow topic, companies, date window and permitted sources. “Everything about competitors” makes it difficult to tell whether the output is complete or relevant.
  2. Make one record per event. Group repeated coverage without deleting its links. Preserve the announcement date separately from publication and retrieval dates. A later rewrite does not make the underlying event new.
  3. Label the evidence. Was it a company statement, a customer account or a report based on the same release? More URLs do not automatically mean more independent evidence. An independent source can still be wrong; provenance is not a reliability score.
  4. Separate interpretation. Put “may reduce setup friction” in an analyst-implication field, not the fact field. Give the reviewer the observation that prompted the hypothesis and what would strengthen or weaken it.
  5. Name a proportionate next step. A question for customer interviews may be justified where a roadmap reversal is not. Assign an owner if the team chooses to investigate. Do not send a digest, edit a roadmap or contact a competitor automatically.

Review one week's source packet manually and compare accepted event records, missed relevant items, duplicate inflation, corrections and actual spend. The article has not measured these outcomes for either product.

Repeated computation is not repeated confirmation

Super's open-source cache proxy stores eligible successful responses and reuses them for matching canonical requests; misses go upstream. Its pinned implementation is inspectable. A cache hit avoids another model call, not all browser or infrastructure costs.

For a weekly brief, retrieve current source material and preserve it in the actual request, or bypass caching. Metadata is excluded from the default cache key, so a new week label only in metadata does not refresh the factual context. A cached summary is not a new source and never adds independent corroboration.

The proxy does not itself monitor websites, verify a rollout, send a newsletter or establish that a workflow completed. Those require separate execution and evidence.
