A whole inverse from one correction

For this unit-vector update, Sherman–Morrison gives M inverse = I - [a / (1 + a)] u u transpose, provided 1 + a is nonzero. The solution changes only along u: x = b - [a / (1 + a)] u(u transpose b).

The component perpendicular to u is unchanged. The component parallel to u is multiplied by 1 / (1 + a). Positive a suppresses that component; negative a above -1 amplifies it. At a = 0 there is no update.

The denominator is a warning

The eigenvalues of M are 1 and 1 + a. At a = -1, M loses the u direction, so no inverse exists. A particular right-hand side may still admit infinitely many solutions if it is perpendicular to u; otherwise no solution exists. This studio reports the missing inverse rather than selecting a pseudoinverse solution.

The numerical engine builds the formula with matrix products and checks its solution against a fresh QR factorization. Agreement on this tiny example is not a general stability guarantee for inverse updates. In larger systems, reuse a factorization and monitor residuals instead of forming inverses unnecessarily.
