Balance by node count

L = D - W
RatioCut(S) = cut(S, complement) times (1/|S| + 1/(6-|S|))

W is the symmetric similarity matrix and D contains weighted degrees. The second-smallest Laplacian eigenvalue and its eigenvector solve a continuous relaxation of the two-way RatioCut problem. Turning real coordinates into a discrete partition is a separate step.

This page sorts the six vector coordinates and evaluates all five nonempty prefixes, keeping the lowest RatioCut. The exact comparison enumerates 31 distinct partitions, placing node A in one group to avoid counting complements twice. A spectral sweep is a heuristic: its score cannot beat the exact optimum, but can exceed it.

When the vector is not unique

Repeated second eigenvalues permit different choices of eigenvector within the same eigenspace. Cycle and star presets expose this ambiguity. Tied coordinates within 1e-10 use node order; a chosen prefix can split a tied group. The output is one library-selected sweep, not a unique clustering.

A disconnected graph has a zero second eigenvalue. This tool withholds its spectral sweep rather than presenting one arbitrary null-space vector as a definitive partition. The exact enumeration still runs. Zero-weight edges are absent; all weights are nonnegative.

ml-matrix supplies symmetric eigendecomposition. Connectivity uses positive edges; eigenvalue multiplicity is reported at tolerance 1e-8. This is unnormalized RatioCut, not degree-normalized Ncut. The tiny exhaustive comparison is educational and does not scale to large datasets. JSON includes W, L, eigenpairs, every sweep and the exact result.
