An evidence design simulator

Every consequential action needs a proof packet.

A confident answer is not the same as an authorized action. Assemble the evidence an agent needs before it changes the world, then see where the packet still breaks.

Choose the proof

Turn on only what the agent can actually establish. The goal is not to collect every possible fact; it is to cover the claims that make this action safe and authorized.

“Send the revised contract to the external partner and mark the deal ready.”
SourceThe contract version is identified and traceable.
ScopeThe requested recipient and permitted action are explicit.
AuthorizationA person or policy grants this exact external side effect.
OutcomeThe system can verify delivery and the resulting state.

The packet is incomplete.

The agent knows what it wants to send, but it cannot yet prove that the external side effect is authorized or observable.

Evidence should travel with the action.

Agents become safer when the decision and its justification share a boundary. A proof packet makes missing authority visible before a tool call, and makes the result inspectable after it returns.

Prove the object

Identify the exact source, version, recipient, and scope. Vague nouns are not permission to act.

Prove the authority

Separate “the user asked” from “the user authorized this side effect.” The latter must cover the actual operation.

Prove the result

Without an observable outcome, an agent cannot honestly claim completion or safely choose a recovery path.
