Combine signals carefully.

An agent can have five observations and still have one piece of evidence repeated five times. Aggregation is useful only when weighting reflects quality, independence, and freshness.

More signals do not automatically mean more certainty. Ask what each signal adds before letting it move the decision.

Weight provenance

Give more influence to signals with clear origin, stable semantics, and a track record that matches the decision.

Discount dependence

Correlated observations should not masquerade as independent confirmation. Shared inputs mean shared blind spots.

Keep dissent visible

Retain outliers and conflicts so an aggregate remains a summary of evidence, not an eraser of uncertainty.
