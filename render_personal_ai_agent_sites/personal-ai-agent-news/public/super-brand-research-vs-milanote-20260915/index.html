Super vs Milanote

Brand research before the moodboard. For freelance designers, a useful brief needs two different things: evidence about the business and a visual direction the client can react to. Do not mistake one for the other.

Publisher: Super Powers AI, a product discussed here. Official sources checked September 15, 2026. No comparative benchmark or integration test was performed.

The short recommendation

Choose Milanote when the work is visual organization and feedback. It supports mixed-media boards, a web clipper, comments, read-only sharing and PDF export. Its product page shows the actual visual workspace below. Milanote moodboarding.

Consider Super for the upstream research brief. Its public product describes automated research, browsers and cloud sandboxes. A proposed use here is gathering authorized public sources and drafting a cited comparison before a designer curates references. That is a workflow proposal, not proof of a Milanote connector or autonomous board creation. Super product documentation.

These can be complementary tools. A polished arrangement does not verify a market claim; a factual brief does not select the right art direction.

A bounded brief for a real design engagement

  1. Agree on the question. For example: "Which positioning claims appear on three named competitors' current homepages?" Do not expand this into a market-share study. Ask the client to identify the intended audience, region and relevant competitors.
  2. Collect a compact evidence packet. For each observation, keep the exact URL, observation date, short excerpt and page context. Separate what a company says about itself from independently established facts. A missing claim on a homepage is not proof that the company lacks the capability.
  3. Write implications as hypotheses. "Two sampled brands emphasize speed" can suggest a workshop question. It cannot establish what every customer values. Keep interviews, client preferences and your visual interpretation in distinct fields.
  4. Curate the visual direction. Move chosen references and short notes into the client's visual workspace manually unless an integration has actually been tested. Keep source attribution attached. Ask the client to approve a direction, not to certify the research merely by liking the board.

Prerequisites: an agreed competitor list, permission to use supplied client material, a place to store the source packet, and a named reviewer. Do not submit confidential strategy or credentials to a new tool before checking its terms and the client's requirements. Do not copy competitors' creative work into deliverables as if it were licensed client artwork.

What the computer-use cache actually establishes

Super's public computer-use-cache repository is inspectable technology. In the pinned implementation, normalized request content is hashed to look up a stored response. Eligible successful JSON responses can be stored; misses go upstream, and expiry and bypass controls exist. Stream misses are forwarded without storage.

This is response reuse, not evidence that an app action happened again. A hit does not revisit a competitor's website or validate a new positioning claim. Metadata is ignored by default, so changing only a date in metadata is not a reliable freshness strategy. For this workflow, supply fresh source content or bypass reuse when a fresh check is required. We have not measured hit rate, speed or cost for brand research, and this source inspection does not establish every deployment's cache configuration.

Costs and the first trial

Milanote's free tier lists 100 notes, images or links, 10 file uploads and unlimited shared boards. Its individual paid plan lists $9.99 per month billed annually or $12.50 billed monthly. Check the current plans page before purchase.

Super describes wallet-based pay-as-you-go use with optional plans. That is not directly comparable to a visual workspace seat price, and no fixed per-brief cost was verified here. Start with one small public-source brief, record actual charges and review time, and stop if the evidence trail is weaker than your manual process.

For a fair trial, use the same three competitors and the same research question. Check whether every observation opens to the correct source, whether stale pages are flagged, and whether interpretations are labeled. Evaluate visual presentation separately. Prefer Milanote alone if you already have the research and only need to organize it; consider a combined workflow if research collection is the bottleneck.

Keep the handoff small

Deliver one page of observed claims, one page of unanswered questions and a separately reviewed set of visual references. Exclude unsupported customer preferences. The useful outcome is a better-informed design conversation, not a bigger pile of screenshots.
