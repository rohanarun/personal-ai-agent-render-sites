Super vs Peerspace

How to shortlist a workshop venue without losing the questions that still need an answer.

For independent workshop organizers. Published by Super Powers AI, a product discussed here. Sources checked September 15, 2026. No venue inspection, booking, integration test or comparative benchmark performed.

Start with the booking tool when booking is the job

Peerspace is the documented specialist for venue discovery and booking. It offers a workshop category and searches by activity, place and time. Its marketplace connects organizers with hosts. Use the listing and booking process to check the actual event, not a summary detached from it. Peerspace and its workshop directory.

Super is a proposed research assistant in this workflow. Its public product describes automated research, browsers and cloud sandboxes. It could help turn approved public sources into a comparison of requirements and unanswered questions. This article does not establish a Peerspace integration, guaranteed live availability or the ability to make a booking on your behalf. Super product documentation.

If you already know the area and your needs fit the marketplace filters, start with Peerspace alone. Consider extra research assistance when the work involves comparing scattered venue information against a detailed brief. Do not add a second tool merely to reformat three listings.

Write the event specification first

A useful shortlist starts with constraints: city and travel range, date and full access window, attendee count, activity, seating layout, equipment, accessibility requirements and a spending ceiling. "Twenty people" does not tell a host whether you need standing space, classroom desks or a messy hands-on workshop.

  1. Count the whole visit. Include setup and cleanup in the requested time. A three-hour class may need a longer rental window. Check minimum booking duration rather than multiplying the advertised rate by class time.
  2. Record evidence and uncertainty separately. Keep the listing URL, observation date, stated capacity, permitted activity and amenities. Mark an unmentioned feature as unknown, not absent or included.
  3. Ask one consolidated question set. Confirm the exact layout, step-free route and restroom needs, equipment included, cleanup responsibilities and total for the full window. Request clarification through the permitted booking channel; do not send inquiries without authorization.
  4. Compare equivalent offers. Keep date, duration, attendee count and add-ons the same. Review cancellation terms and the final checkout before committing. A shortlist entry is not a held reservation.

Do not infer accessibility from a wide-angle photograph or a generic label. Ask about the specific route and facilities your attendees need, and verify with the venue before making assurances.

Why the hourly rate is not your total

Peerspace says the guest processing fee varies with booking characteristics and is shown during booking. It applies to the subtotal including cleaning fees; the exact amount appears before confirmation. We therefore do not invent a universal percentage. Guest processing fee.

Hosts may charge for cleaning and other described services or circumstances. Review the specific listing and quote for included equipment, add-ons and overtime. Host charges. The useful comparison is the complete quoted event cost, not the smallest number on the search card.

Super describes pay-as-you-go wallet use with optional plans. No per-shortlist price or saving was measured here. Any assistant cost is separate from the venue and should be evaluated alongside the review time it creates or removes.

Do not promote "unknown" to "confirmed"

Fictional example: a listing says "up to 24 guests" and "tables available." You need 20 seated participants, step-free access and a full-window quote. Mark each item only as part of this demonstration; the controls do not verify a real venue.

Even all three confirmations leave the date, booking status and cancellation review separate. The download is a draft, never a reservation or message sent to a host.

What cache reuse does not confirm

The public computer-use-cache contains real inspectable code. In the pinned implementation, normalized requests are hashed for stored-response lookup; eligible successful JSON responses can be reused. Misses go upstream, expiry and bypass controls exist, and streaming misses are not stored.

A response hit does not refresh a venue calendar, obtain a quote or reserve a room. Metadata is ignored by default, so changing only a timestamp there is not a reliable freshness request. Bring fresh source content or bypass reuse when current facts are required, then verify the booking through the actual provider. We measured no hit rate, speedup or cost advantage for this task.

A small pilot beats a sweeping promise

Try three candidate venues for one real specification. Have a human check every listing link, source date and unknown field. Compare that review effort with your normal search process. Keep a source-backed shortlist if it reduces missed questions; discard a polished answer that invents amenities or availability.
