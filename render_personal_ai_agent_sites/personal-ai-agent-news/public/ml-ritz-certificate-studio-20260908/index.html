What the interval promises

For symmetric A and a unit vector u, set r = Au − θu. At least one eigenvalue lies within [θ − ‖r‖₂, θ + ‖r‖₂]. This is a proximity bound, not a statistical confidence interval. It does not identify a particular eigenvalue or guarantee an accurate eigenvector.

In an orthonormal eigenbasis, ‖r‖₂² = Σ(λᵢ − θ)²uᵢ². Since Σuᵢ² = 1, the sum is at least the squared distance to the nearest eigenvalue. The plotted intervals use this direct residual, not an estimated convergence rate.

Inside this experiment

A = diag(1, 2, 5, 9), v = (1, 1, 1, s). QR orthogonalizes [v, Av, …, Aᵐ⁻¹v] into Q. Solving H = QᵀAQ gives H y = θy; lifting u = Qy produces a Ritz pair. Its residual is orthogonal to the trial subspace. At dimension four the whole spectrum is recovered, up to floating-point error.

This tiny powers-and-QR construction illustrates the projection behind spectral methods used in ML. It is not a production Lanczos implementation: explicit powers can become ill-conditioned in larger problems. Symmetry is essential to the stated bound.
