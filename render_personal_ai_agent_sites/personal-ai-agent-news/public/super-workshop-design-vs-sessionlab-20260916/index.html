Super Powers AI vs SessionLab: choose by the job you need done, from outside research to a plan another facilitator can actually deliver.

Choose SessionLab for agenda design. Consider Super for a separately scoped research task when missing external information is the bottleneck. A generated outline is not a rehearsed workshop, and an agent is not a substitute for a facilitation system.

Start with the missing job

SessionLab provides a drag-and-drop agenda builder, timing support and an activity library. Its current offering includes AI assistance, not just manual planning. If your challenge is rearranging blocks, coordinating co-facilitators or exporting a delivery plan, evaluate that native workflow first. Official product overview.

Super advertises cloud browsers, apps and automated research reports. A proposed role is finding public examples relevant to your workshop and returning source-backed notes for review. This article does not demonstrate a SessionLab connector, automatic agenda import or attendance integration. Super product.

From a vague request to a deliverable

Fictional brief: eight new team leads need to practice giving actionable project updates. They have 90 minutes and mixed experience. “Run a communication workshop” is too broad; the concrete outcome is that each participant leaves with a revised update containing progress, a blocker and a clear request.

  1. Confirm the brief with the sponsor. Agree on the intended output, audience, time available and what participants already know. Ask about access needs and avoid collecting unnecessary personal information.
  2. Research only what is missing. A bounded Super assignment could find two public examples of structured status updates, with source links and distinctions between advice and evidence. Do not send confidential participant stories just to get generic examples.
  3. Design backward from practice. Allocate time for an attempt, feedback and revision before adding a presentation. The participant artifact is more informative than whether every slide was shown.
  4. Transfer the approved content into the plan. SessionLab can be the planning home. The transfer here is manual; verify timing, instructions and materials instead of assuming an agent output is ready to run.
  5. Rehearse transitions. Check how long it takes to explain the activity, form pairs and regroup. Keep an optional example you can remove without deleting the practice or close.

Cache the repeat, not the assumption

Super's public computer-use cache exposes concrete implementation. The pinned server uses normalized request keys and stores eligible responses. A hit can avoid another upstream model request; a miss goes upstream. TTL and bypass controls affect reuse, and streaming misses are not stored in this implementation.

A reused response is not proof that a source is still current, a participant has consented or the room setup has been checked. For changing constraints, collect fresh evidence and review the plan. Do not put sensitive participant disclosures in cacheable requests. Public code is not an audit of every production configuration, and no savings benchmark is claimed here.

When the competitor is the better fit

You facilitate regularly with other people

Use a dedicated planning workflow when shared plans, revisions and delivery exports are central. An extra agent may add little if the content is already known.

You are still investigating the topic

A small research assignment may help before planning starts. Require traceable sources and review the substance yourself. Do not treat a polished response as facilitation expertise.

You only need one simple session

A document and a rehearsal may be sufficient. Compare that baseline before paying for either tool. Useful preparation is the goal, not a larger software stack.

Evaluate a trial by whether the resulting plan has clear instructions, realistic transitions and a participant output you can assess. Record revisions required and preparation time. The sample above proves only that its blocks total 90 minutes, not that they will work for your audience.
