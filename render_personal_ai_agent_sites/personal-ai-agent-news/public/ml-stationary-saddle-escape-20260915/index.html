The origin fails the minimum test

For f(x,y) = x² - y², the gradient is (2x, -2y). It vanishes at the origin. But every small nonzero displacement along x raises f, while every small nonzero displacement along y lowers it. There are both higher and lower values arbitrarily close to (0,0). That makes the origin a saddle, not a local minimum.

The Hessian is the diagonal matrix with entries 2 and -2. Its negative eigenvalue supplies a direction of negative curvature. A gradient-norm stopping rule sees zero at the origin; it does not inspect this curvature. These claims follow directly from this quadratic, without a training dataset or fitted parameters.

Two directions, opposite behavior

With fixed learning rate η = 0.1, gradient descent gives x_next = 0.8 x and y_next = 1.2 y. Thus x_k = 0.8^k x_0 and y_k = 1.2^k y_0 in exact arithmetic. One component shrinks while the other grows.

Select At saddle and take eight steps. Nothing moves: an exact zero gradient produces an exact zero update. Select Stable axis, which starts at (0.8,0). The trajectory approaches the saddle without leaving that axis. Then select Perturbation, starting at (0.8,0.1). The small y component grows, so the trajectory eventually moves away even while x contracts.

For a nonzero y_0, decreasing the initial perturbation delays escape rather than eliminating it. The number of steps to reach a magnitude threshold depends logarithmically on that initial magnitude through 1.2^k |y_0|. At y_0 = 0 the behavior is qualitatively different: no y component ever appears in this deterministic update.

Descent can lead away from stationarity

For this learning rate, one step changes the objective by f_next - f = -0.36 x² - 0.44 y². Except at the origin, the value strictly decreases. A trajectory escaping the saddle is therefore not a failure to descend. The growing coordinate lies in a direction where lower values are available.

The gradient norm can initially fall as x contracts and later rise as y grows. A small gradient alone cannot certify a good solution. Conversely, a rising gradient norm does not necessarily mean the objective rose. Inspect the path export to compare objective values and gradients across steps.

This objective is unbounded below. Escaping does not mean finding another minimum: there is no global minimum here. The displayed domain is [-2,2] in each horizontal coordinate. The experiment stops before an update would leave that domain, or at 50 steps. Neither display limit is an optimization convergence claim.

What carries over, and what does not

The distinction between stationarity and local optimality matters in non-convex optimization. Dauphin and colleagues' saddle-point paper studies the broader high-dimensional problem and proposes a second-order approach. This small visualization is not a reproduction of that algorithm or its experiments.

Real training involves many parameters, stochastic gradients, other curvature directions and finite precision. This example adds no noise, uses a fixed rate and has a constant Hessian. It does not prove all training stalls are saddles, nor that perturbing weights always improves a model. It isolates why a first-order stopping signal is insufficient.

The gradients are computed with TensorFlow.js automatic differentiation on the CPU backend. Three.js renders the surface and path. The closed-form recurrences are independent checks on the numerical implementation.
