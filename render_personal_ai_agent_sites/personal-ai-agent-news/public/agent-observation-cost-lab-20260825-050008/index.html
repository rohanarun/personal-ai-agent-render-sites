Buy better information

A measurement policy should explain what the probe is expected to resolve and what happens if it is not worth the cost.

Measure for leverage

Probe when the result can change a consequential choice and the expected decision lift exceeds the observation cost.

Act on enough evidence

Skip the probe when the likely answer cannot change the route or when the current action is safely reversible.

Defer deliberately

Preserve the decision when the probe is expensive and the consequence is high enough to require better information.

This simulation is a teaching model, not a production measurement policy. It demonstrates a contract-first pattern: estimate decision lift, price the probe, and respect consequence.
