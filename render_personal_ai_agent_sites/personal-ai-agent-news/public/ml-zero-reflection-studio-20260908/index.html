Same gain, different phase

The minimum-phase filter is (1 - a z-1)(1 - b z-1), with both zeros inside the unit circle. Reflecting a replaces its factor with (a - z-1), including the gain adjustment. Its zero is now 1/a.

For real a, |1 - a exp(-i omega)| equals |a - exp(-i omega)|. Each reflection preserves the full magnitude response, not only the plotted FFT bins. Reflecting both reverses the three impulse coefficients.

The ratio of a reflected factor to its original is allpass. Its extra group delay is (1 - a squared) / (1 + a squared - 2a cos(omega)), which is positive. Group delay is minus the phase derivative; it is not the arrival time of an individual sample.

Among causal responses with the same magnitude, the minimum-phase response has at least as much cumulative squared energy through every sample. The table demonstrates that property for this family. Equal magnitude does not mean equal waveform timing or a uniquely identified filter.

The 128-point FFT uses fft.js. No zero lies on the unit circle, so the delay calculation has no spectral singularity in this experiment.
