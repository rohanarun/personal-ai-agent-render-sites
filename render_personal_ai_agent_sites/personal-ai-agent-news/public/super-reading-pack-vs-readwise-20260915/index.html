Super vs Readwise Reader

Build a discussion pack without letting the summary replace the reading.

For adult learning groups and facilitators. Published by Super Powers AI, a product discussed here. Official sources checked September 15, 2026. No comparative benchmark or integration test performed.

Choose the tool for the work you actually have

Readwise Reader is the documented fit for collecting and reading material. It handles articles, PDFs, EPUBs and newsletters, with highlighting and annotations. Ghostreader can answer questions and explain language. The product also describes highlight exports through Readwise to note-taking tools. It is not merely a passive bookmark folder. Reader product page.

Super's proposed role is a bounded facilitation draft. Its public product describes automated research, browsers and cloud sandboxes. A possible task is turning an approved source list into a reading order, a short glossary and questions with passage references. That is not evidence of access to your Reader library, group collaboration features or a native integration. Super product documentation.

If you need a better personal reading environment, start with Reader. If you already have sources and need a facilitator's draft, test the drafting workflow separately. A group should not have to adopt a paid app merely to open the assigned original links.

Make the pack smaller than the source pile

  1. Choose one discussion question. For example: "What evidence would change the author's conclusion?" A focused objective is easier to review than an unrestricted topic overview.
  2. Assign a small, accessible source set. Use links participants can lawfully access, identify required versus optional reading and keep edition or publication dates. Test links outside your signed-in session; your subscription does not necessarily give everyone access.
  3. Preserve three layers. Keep a short attributed passage, a clearly labeled paraphrase and your discussion prompt distinct. Include page or section references. Do not place paraphrases in quotation marks or silently merge different authors.
  4. Check disagreement at the source. When two readings appear to conflict, ask whether they address the same population, question and time period. Do not force a consensus the authors did not claim.
  5. Review before distributing. The facilitator checks every premise and removes unsupported claims. Share links and a concise original guide rather than redistributing entire copyrighted texts without permission.

Prerequisites: authorized access to the readings, a facilitator, a clear learning question and time for source checking. Do not upload private group notes or participants' personal disclosures unless the group and relevant terms permit it.

Pricing: Reader is not the Lite plan

The official pricing page lists full Readwise, including Reader, at $9.99 per month billed annually ($119.88 for 12 months), or $12.99 monthly. A 30-day trial is listed. Lite is a different offering; do not use its lower price as the Reader price. Confirm the current plan and terms before subscribing.

Super describes wallet-based pay-as-you-go use with optional plans. No per-pack cost was measured here. Compare actual charges and facilitator correction time, not the number of paragraphs produced. Reader's own AI features may already meet your needs; a second assistant should earn its place through a better reviewed result.

The cache reuses responses, not understanding

Super's public computer-use-cache exposes an implementation you can inspect. The pinned source hashes normalized request content, returns eligible stored responses and forwards misses upstream. It includes expiry and bypass controls; streaming misses are not stored.

A cache hit does not re-read a revised article, check permission or verify a paraphrase. Metadata is ignored by default, so changing only an edition date there does not reliably refresh the result. Supply the actual revised content or bypass reuse when needed, and review against the correct edition. No cache hit rate, speedup or cost saving was measured for this reading-group workflow.

A fair one-session trial

Use the same small source set and discussion objective for your existing method and an assisted draft. Check whether participants can open the readings, whether every quoted passage is accurate and whether each question has a defensible premise. Record how many corrections the facilitator makes. Do not infer improved learning from a prettier handout or an enthusiastic discussion.
