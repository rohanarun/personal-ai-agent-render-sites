The Christoffel variational principle

Energy means one half of the integral of q(x)^2 from -1 to 1, the mean squared amplitude under the uniform probability measure. Among polynomials of degree at most n with q(t) = 1, the minimum is 1 / K_n(t,t), where K_n is the reproducing kernel for that polynomial space.

In monomial coordinates v(x) = [1, x, ..., x^n], the moment matrix has G(i,j) = 0 for odd i+j and 1/(i+j+1) otherwise, with zero-based indices. The optimal coefficients are G^(-1)v(t) / (v(t)^T G^(-1)v(t)). ml-matrix solves this small system; the degree is capped at six because monomial moment matrices become ill-conditioned.

The added term r(x) = perturbation (x-t) vanishes at the pin. Optimality makes its inner product with the optimum zero. Consequently the extra energy is perturbation^2 (1/3 + t^2), not an approximation from the plotted samples.

This is a finite-dimensional minimum-norm interpolation example relevant to kernel methods. The polynomial may be negative or exceed one away from the pin; it is not a probability density. All integrals here use exact polynomial moments in floating-point arithmetic.
