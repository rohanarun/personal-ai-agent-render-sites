Prune the plan before it acts.

An agent rarely fails because it cannot imagine options. It fails when weak options survive too long. Explore the small control loop that turns competing hypotheses into one testable next move.

Good reasoning is not endless branching. It is a disciplined reduction of uncertainty, with evidence and risk making the cut.

Rule of thumb: preserve alternatives until the next observation can actually distinguish them.

Generate, then rank

Branching creates coverage; ranking creates focus. Keep the candidate set visible so the cut is explainable, not magical.

Pay for information

A verification step is valuable when its expected information gain exceeds its latency, money, or tool-call cost.

Stop with a postcondition

Pruning is complete only when the next action has a measurable success condition and a recovery path if it misses.
