Super vs Sortly

Research what an item is before deciding how to count it.

A supplier page says “six-pack.” The photograph shows one jar. A second page describes a different size. An assistant can help organize those discrepancies, but a fluent answer does not establish which product will arrive. For an independent retailer, product research and stock control are related jobs, not interchangeable software categories.

The short decision

Choose Sortly when the problem is recording and finding physical inventory. Its official product page describes item photos, custom fields, location-based folders, barcode and QR scanning, low-stock alerts, cloud synchronization and PDF/CSV reports. Those are inventory responsibilities. This comparison does not position Super as a replacement for them. Source: Sortly.

Consider Super for the work before the record: researching public product pages, organizing supplied information and drafting questions for a buyer to review. Super's public site describes browsers, cloud sandboxes and automated research. A practical research-to-record handoff

  1. Define the candidate precisely. Start with the manufacturer name, model or SKU, size, color and supplier URL. Keep supplier SKUs separate from manufacturer identifiers. A similar photo is not an identity match.
  2. Ask for evidence, not a synthesized catalog entry. A proposed Super request: “Compare these supplied product pages. For each claim, retain its source URL, the exact variant and the date observed. Separate direct statements from inference. List contradictions and missing details. Do not order or change records.”
  3. Resolve buying-unit versus counting-unit. Have a person confirm whether a case contains six saleable units and whether the quoted amount is per case or per unit. Define the unit in the inventory record before entering a quantity. Do not treat the number in a marketing title as confirmation.
  4. Review before transfer. A buyer checks the current source or obtains supplier confirmation, then creates the record using their existing inventory process. This article has not tested an import or sync path between the products.

Keep uncertain specifications in the review draft, not in a customer-facing promise. For certification, safety or suitability claims, obtain the applicable documentation for the exact model and seek qualified review where needed; an assistant's summary is not certification.

What the cache actually establishes

Super publishes Computer-Use Cache. In the pinned proxy implementation reviewed here, normalized request content is hashed; eligible successful JSON responses can be stored and replayed. A miss goes upstream. Expiry and bypass controls exist, and streaming misses are not stored.

That is inspectable technology, not evidence that this retailer workflow has been deployed. A cache hit avoids another upstream model call for that response; it does not prove a fresh supplier visit or a new physical stock count. Other execution and infrastructure costs can remain. Metadata is ignored by default, so putting a new observation date only in metadata is not a reliable freshness strategy. Include newly fetched source content in the substantive request or use an appropriate bypass. The public proxy alone does not establish every production cache behavior.

Budget for different jobs

Sortly's pricing page currently lists a free plan with 100 unique items and one user license. Item quantity does not count toward that unique-item limit. Paid plans have separate limits and the page advertises an annual promotion; verify the billing term, renewal amount and required features before purchasing. Check current Sortly plans.

Super advertises pay-as-you-go wallet funding and optional paid plans. This is not an apples-to-apples inventory subscription comparison: budget research and human review separately from stock software. No cost or time saving was measured for this workflow.

When not to add an assistant

If the supplier already provides an unambiguous product sheet and your real problem is misplaced stock, begin with the inventory process. If you need retail point-of-sale or ecommerce synchronization, verify that specific integration with the vendor instead of inferring it from “inventory management.” If public sources disagree, the next step may be a supplier email, not more summarization.

Super is worth evaluating when gathering and comparing scattered evidence is the bottleneck. Test a small set of non-sensitive products, check every resulting claim and measure correction effort. Keep purchase approval and authoritative quantities with people and systems already responsible for them.
