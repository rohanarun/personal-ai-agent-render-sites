Order is enough in one dimension

For equally weighted samples of the same size, the squared Wasserstein distance is the average squared difference between sorted positions. It is W2 squared, not W2 itself; the distance is its square root. The POT one-dimensional transport documentation gives the equivalent inverse-CDF integral and sorting formulation.

W2 squared = (1/6) sum of (source at rank i - destination at rank i) squared

The saving from one uncrossing

Take source positions a < b and destinations c < d. Matching a to d and b to c costs more than matching a to c and b to d. Expanding the squares gives the difference below. Every strict inversion can therefore be removed profitably.

[(a-d)^2 + (b-c)^2 - (a-c)^2 - (b-d)^2] / 6 = 2(b-a)(d-c) / 6

Equal destination positions can give multiple equally good labeled matchings. This rank rule is for a one-dimensional convex distance cost, not arbitrary cost tables or multidimensional points. Unequal masses may split across destinations and are outside this six-parcel experiment.
