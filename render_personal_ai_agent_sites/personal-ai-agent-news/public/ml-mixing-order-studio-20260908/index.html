Every mix has a matrix witness

A pair changes from (a,b) to ((1-t)a+tb, ta+(1-t)b). Its matrix is nonnegative with every row and column summing to one. Products retain these properties.

A vector majorizes another when a doubly stochastic matrix maps the first to the second. Equivalently, every descending cumulative sum of the second is at most that of the first, with equal totals. See John Watrous, chapter 4, Definition 4.29 and Theorem 4.30.

For probabilities, mixing cannot decrease Shannon entropy or increase the squared norm: apply concavity of -x log2(x) and convexity of x squared to each mixed entry, then add. A swap leaves both unchanged. Higher entropy alone is not a complete test for majorization.

This is a finite probability-vector experiment, not a model-training claim. The initial vector is (0.55, 0.25, 0.15, 0.05). Undo restores saved state, not an inverse mixing operation. Export includes every state and the cumulative doubly stochastic matrix. At 40 actions, undo or reset before adding another.
