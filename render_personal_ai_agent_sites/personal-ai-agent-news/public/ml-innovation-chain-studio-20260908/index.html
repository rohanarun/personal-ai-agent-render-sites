Dense dependence, local precision

Green is positive, pink negative, white zero. Colors are normalized to the largest absolute entry of the displayed matrix; compare numbers through the export.

With negative rho, neighboring observations prefer opposite signs. An alternating sequence may then cost less energy than a smooth one. With positive rho, the preference reverses.

A stationary AR(1) model

The model is X[t] = rho X[t-1] + noise[t], with independent zero-mean Gaussian noise of variance 1 - rho squared and a stationary initial value of variance 1. Since |rho| is below 1, C[i,j] = rho to the power |i-j|.

W maps an observation vector to innovations: its first diagonal entry is 1; later diagonal entries are 1 / sqrt(1 - rho squared), with -rho / sqrt(1 - rho squared) immediately below. Therefore W C W transpose = I, and C inverse = W transpose W. The precision is tridiagonal even though distant values remain correlated.

For this Gaussian model, zero precision entries encode conditional independence given the other observations. They do not imply zero marginal correlation. The energy shown is a quadratic-form term, not a complete negative log likelihood: comparing models also requires the log determinant and normalization terms. Editing rho does not fit it to these observations.
