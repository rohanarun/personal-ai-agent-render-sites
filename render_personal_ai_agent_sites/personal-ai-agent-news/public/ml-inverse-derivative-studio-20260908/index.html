Differentiate the identity, not a scalar mnemonic

Write B = A-1. Differentiating BA = I along E gives (DB[E])A + BE = 0. Multiplying on the right by B yields DB[E] = -BEB. Moving E to the end instead gives -B²E, which agrees only when A and E commute.

For symmetric A and E, the correct derivative is symmetric. The wrong ordering can destroy that symmetry. Finite differences offer a separate check: forward uses [inv(A+hE)−inv(A)]/h; central uses [inv(A+hE)−inv(A−hE)]/(2h). Their truncation errors are respectively O(h) and O(h²) near zero, before floating-point cancellation dominates.

This identity matters when differentiating linear algebra in ML. Explicit inverses here expose the small example; production linear solves usually avoid forming an inverse.
