Logarithms turn the echo into a series

The response is h[0] = 1 and h[d] = a. Its spectrum is H[k] = 1 + a exp(-i 2 pi k d / 128). The real cepstrum is inverse FFT(ln|H|), computed here with fft.js. Since a is below one, |H| stays above zero and no logarithm floor is needed.

For an infinite frequency grid, expanding ln|1 + a exp(-i omega d)| gives coefficients (-1)m+1am / (2m) at both +md and -md, for positive integers m. The first pair is positive; the second is negative. These are delay harmonics, not additional echoes in h.

Sampling the spectrum at 128 bins folds these infinitely many coefficients modulo 128. Contributions can combine, so the displayed first peak need not equal exactly a/2. The mirrored half is exported; the plot shows only 0–64.

Cepstral representations are used in speech modeling, but this is an isolated noiseless echo response. It is not an MFCC implementation, a pitch tracker, or a claim that real recordings always reveal their echo delay.
