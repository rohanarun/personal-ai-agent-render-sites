A launch graphic needs a decision, not a mood

Consider a fictional scheduling product that now supports reusable meeting templates. “The future of effortless teamwork” supplies a mood but no testable message. “Reuse your meeting setup” identifies the change without claiming that every team saves a specific amount of time.

  1. Name one audience. Existing admins configuring recurring meetings need different context from people who have never heard of the product.
  2. Write one supported claim. Separate the shipped feature from a hoped-for outcome. A reusable template is not evidence of a 50% productivity gain.
  3. Attach the evidence. Link a reviewed release note, screenshot version or product-owner confirmation. Record availability restrictions rather than hiding them in the design handoff.
  4. Choose one next action. “Open the template settings” and “book a sales call” imply different journeys. Verify that the destination actually supports the chosen action.
  5. Specify the deliverable. Agree channel, dimensions, file format, deadline, owner and accessibility review. Confirm current channel specifications instead of relying on a remembered universal image size.
  6. Review message and rendering separately. A product owner checks accuracy; a designer checks hierarchy and legibility at actual display size. Neither check substitutes for the other.

Give Super a bounded assignment
Using these approved release notes and product screenshots, prepare a creative brief for existing admins. Separate shipped capabilities from expected benefits. Attach a source reference to every factual claim, list eligibility questions, and propose a single next action. Do not invent performance numbers, create a customer quote, or publish anything.
Provide the permitted source set and the person who can resolve conflicts. When two notes disagree about rollout, the useful result is an explicit question, not an assistant quietly choosing the more attractive version. Keep confidential roadmap details out of public copy and apply your organization's data-sharing rules before supplying documents.
Once the brief is reviewed, transfer it to Canva or your existing design process. This page does not access either account, render a final graphic, schedule a post, or collect approvals.
