Translation first, orientation next
Each point set is centered by its own mean. The fit minimizes the sum of squared distances between matched centered rows under an orthogonal transform. After fitting, the target mean is restored. Orthogonal transforms preserve source distances, so this operation cannot stretch a shape to repair a scale mismatch.
For row matrices X and Y, the singular value decomposition of X^T Y is U S V^T. With reflections allowed, R = U V^T. For a proper rotation, the final singular direction is sign-corrected when needed so det(R) = +1. This studio uses ml-matrix for the SVD and reports the affine row-vector map source R + translation.
A mirror is a modeling choice
An orthogonal matrix may have determinant -1, reversing orientation. Allowing that can perfectly align a reflected embedding, but may be invalid for a physical rigid body. The constraint should come from the problem, not from whichever choice produces the smaller error.
The RMS residual is sqrt(sum of squared point distances / 4), not a per-coordinate RMSE. A collapsed target has no orientation information; rank-deficient cross-matrices can leave the orientation ambiguous. The displayed fit is then one numerical solution, not a uniquely identified transformation.
Alignment does not establish equivalence
Paired embeddings may differ only by coordinate orientation, which motivates alignment before comparison. But a low residual on four anchors does not prove semantic equivalence on unseen points. Correct correspondences are an assumption. Incorrect matches, nonlinear changes, and scale differences can survive the best rotation.
The presets are synthetic demonstrations. No matching, scaling, dimensionality reduction, or learned model is included. Exporting the inputs and fitted transform makes the exact comparison reproducible.
