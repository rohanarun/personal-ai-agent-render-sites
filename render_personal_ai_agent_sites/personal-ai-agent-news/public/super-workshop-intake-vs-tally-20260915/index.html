Super vs Tally

Collecting answers is not the same as having a complete workshop plan.

A facilitator preparing a beginner software workshop needs to know whether participants can bring a laptop, which setup steps are feasible and who needs a follow-up. A blank answer to "Can you bring a laptop?" does not mean yes. It also does not mean no. That small distinction can decide whether the session starts smoothly or spends its first half resolving assumptions.

Use Tally for the collection workflow

Tally is a form builder with a document-like editor. It supports conditional logic, answer piping and integrations such as Google Sheets and webhooks. It can collect structured answers and adapt the form to prior responses. You do not need an assistant merely to create branching questions or move submissions into an existing workflow. Official Tally overview.

Tally's Free offering includes unlimited forms and submissions subject to fair-use guidelines. The pricing page lists conditional logic and several integrations among free features. Pro adds features including custom domains, collaboration and partial submissions; Business adds data-retention controls. Check the selected monthly or yearly plan before purchase. Consider Super for a reviewed planning brief

Super Powers AI publicly describes browser and cloud-computer capabilities. A proposed use is reviewing a redacted set of workshop answers against the facilitator's requirements, grouping unresolved questions and preparing a follow-up draft. This article has not tested a Tally connection, imported submissions or sent messages. Super product description.

The request should say: "Preserve each answer's meaning. Distinguish explicit yes, explicit no, unknown and conflicting records. Quote the evidence for each planning conclusion. Do not infer missing answers or make commitments to participants." The organizer must review the brief before changing the workshop plan.

Design the intake around decisions

  1. Ask only what changes preparation. If laptop availability affects whether you need shared machines, ask it plainly. Avoid collecting extra personal details because a form builder makes it easy. Explain why the answer is needed and who will use it.
  2. Offer an honest uncertainty choice. "Not sure yet" may be more useful than forcing a yes or no. Keep an unanswered field distinct from that explicit response: one person expressed uncertainty; the other supplied no answer.
  3. Branch to a practical follow-up. If a participant cannot bring equipment, ask only the next necessary question. Do not turn a simple intake into a long survey. Preview each branch with sample answers before sharing the form.
  4. Preserve the original response. A summary should point back to its source record and timestamp. If a participant updates an answer, retain the distinction between the earlier response and the currently confirmed one. Do not silently merge contradictory records.
  5. Resolve gaps with a person. Ask the participant or designated organizer for clarification. A model can draft the question, but it cannot supply the answer. Keep promises about spare equipment, setup support or attendance outside an unreviewed summary.

What can the organizer conclude?

Fictional records for a laptop question. The selector does not collect a registration or validate a real submission.

Partial data deserves an explicit label
An unfinished form is not necessarily the participant's final intention. If you enable partial-submission capture, decide how that information will be labeled, reviewed and retained. Do not count it as a confirmed registration simply because a row exists. Likewise, a submission count is not a verified attendance count.
For the workshop, a useful brief can have three lists: confirmed preparation needs, unresolved answers and organizer decisions. Each item needs a source and an owner for follow-up. Keep participants' contact details out of a broadly shared summary when they are not necessary for its audience.
The cache does not fill in missing answers
The public computer-use-cache repository contains inspectable response-reuse code. Its pinned proxy implementation keys normalized requests and can store eligible successful JSON responses, with expiration and bypass behavior. A hit can return stored output without another upstream model call; a miss goes upstream.
That does not establish a fresh submission, permission to contact someone or the meaning of a blank field. This source review is not a measured production hit rate, task-replay proof or total-savings benchmark. Keep the latest actual records available when reviewing any reused planning response.
Choose the part you are missing
Choose Tally first when the bottleneck is collecting answers reliably through a form. Consider Super only when interpreting approved material into a reviewable planning brief remains a separate task. If your existing spreadsheet or form workflow already makes unknowns clear, you may not need another tool.
Start with a small, redacted sample containing a missing answer and a contradiction. Accept the assistant's brief only if it preserves both instead of smoothing them into certainty. For turning the reviewed plan into assigned work, see Super versus Trello for event follow-through.
