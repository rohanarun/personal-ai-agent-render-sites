The baseline comes from the margins

Expected agreement p[e] is A's yes rate times B's yes rate, plus their no-rate product. Observed agreement p[o] is the diagonal count divided by the total. Kappa = (p[o] - p[e]) / (1 - p[e]).

Same agreement, different baseline

The balanced preset has 80% observed agreement and 50% expected agreement, giving kappa 0.6. The mostly-yes preset still has 80% observed agreement, but its 90% yes margins imply 82% expected agreement. Kappa is then about -0.111.

Chance adjustment is a model

The baseline imagines independent label draws at the observed marginal rates. It does not prove that either person actually guesses randomly. Agreement also does not establish correctness: both labelers could share the same systematic mistake.

Undefined is not perfect

If both labelers use only the same single label, observed and expected agreement are both one. The formula is 0/0, so kappa is undefined. No observations also give no estimate. This demo provides no confidence interval or universal quality threshold.
