A curve above its chord

Log determinant is concave on the positive definite matrices, as shown in Boyd and Vandenberghe, Convex Optimization, Section 3.1.5, pages 73-74. Averaging matrices before taking log determinant therefore gives at least the average of their log determinants.

log det((1-t)A + tB) >= (1-t) log det A + t log det B

Here both endpoint log determinants are zero. The whole curve is the concavity gap. Equal matrices make the gap zero for every weight; distinct matrices give a positive gap at interior weights. The endpoints always have zero gap.

What the area means

The ellipse for a positive definite X has semiaxes equal to the square roots of its eigenvalues, hence area pi times sqrt(det X). Our displayed area ratio is therefore exp(gap / 2). It can exceed one even though neither endpoint ellipse encloses the other.

These are matrix ellipses, not a claim that averaging two probability densities produces a Gaussian. Matrix averaging is also not the same as averaging points along the ellipse boundaries. Natural logarithms are used; all matrices here remain strictly positive definite.
