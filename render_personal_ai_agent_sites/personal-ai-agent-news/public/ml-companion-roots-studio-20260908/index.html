Companion matrix C

One characteristic polynomial

For p(z) = z3 + az2 + bz + c, this companion matrix has columns (0,1,0), (0,0,1), and (-c,-b,-a). Expanding det(zI - C) gives p(z), so its three eigenvalues are the polynomial's roots, with multiplicity.

The discriminant is a2b2 - 4b3 - 4a3c - 27c2 + 18abc. For real coefficients, a positive value means three distinct real roots; a negative value means one real root and a nonreal conjugate pair. Zero signals a repeated root.

Near a repeated root, a tiny coefficient change can move roots noticeably. A small polynomial residual does not by itself certify a small root error. At exact multiplicity, floating-point eigenvalues may split slightly; the classification here comes from the coefficients, not an imaginary-part cutoff.

Roots are computed locally with ml-matrix's general eigensolver. This is a small numerical experiment, not a certified polynomial root isolator.
