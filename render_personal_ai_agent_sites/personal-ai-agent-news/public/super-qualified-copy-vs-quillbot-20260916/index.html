A claim-preserving editing workflow

Imagine a small nonprofit preparing a newsletter from a public program announcement. The source says a pilot may expand, but the draft announces a confirmed expansion. Shorter copy has accidentally become a stronger promise.

  1. Keep a source record. Retain the official URL, publication date, relevant passage and date checked. A link alone can hide an outdated page or an unrelated section.
  2. Mark the protected meaning. Identify who, what, when, uncertainty, scope and conditions. These are constraints on the rewrite, not words to delete for a cleaner tone.
  3. Ask for a constrained edit. Request plain language while preserving those constraints. Provide only material you are permitted to share. Do not add private client information when a public excerpt is sufficient.
  4. Compare claims, not just wording. Check each rewritten sentence against the source. A new number, broader population, causal claim or firmer commitment needs support.
  5. Approve the actual final copy. Recheck after headlines and captions are added. A careful paragraph does not rescue an overconfident subject line. Keep attribution where the source is being paraphrased.

Prerequisites are access to the source, permission to reuse the material and an editor who understands the context. If a claim remains ambiguous, retain the uncertainty or ask the source owner instead of filling the gap.

What the cache actually reuses

Super's public computer-use cache is inspectable technology. The pinned implementation normalizes request keys and stores eligible responses. Hits can avoid an upstream model request; misses go upstream. TTL and bypass controls affect reuse. Streaming misses are not stored by this implementation.

A cache hit does not mean a source was reread, a citation was checked or a rewrite was approved. If the source changes, obtain fresh evidence and manage reuse accordingly. Public implementation is not proof of every production setting, semantic accuracy or zero total execution cost. Do not put confidential drafts into a cache without reviewing storage and access controls.

Choose by the work left to do

The source is ready; the prose needs editing

Start with a focused writing tool such as QuillBot, or edit manually. Use a small excerpt and compare meaning carefully. Do not add an agent simply to add another step.

The source is missing or scattered

Evaluate a scoped research task with Super, requiring official links and unresolved questions. Review the evidence before rewriting. This is a proposed workflow, not a measured result.

The claim has important consequences

Escalate to an appropriate human reviewer. Neither grammar scores nor generated confidence establishes factual correctness. Follow any applicable publication or workplace rules for AI assistance.

A useful trial counts unsupported changes and review time, not just words produced. Keep a few difficult source passages as a repeatable evaluation set. If checking the rewrite costs more than editing directly, the manual path may be the better choice.
