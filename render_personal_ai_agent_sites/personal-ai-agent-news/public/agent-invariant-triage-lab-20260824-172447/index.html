The rule matters, but the evidence is not fresh enough to trust a confident action. Spend one step checking the assumption.

A policy is only useful when its trigger is legible.

Imagine an agent with three instructions: preserve user consent, avoid irreversible changes, and finish the task quickly. In a stable world, they align. Under new evidence, they compete. The agent needs a small protocol for deciding which promise to protect first.

This lab compresses that protocol into four signals. Strong invariants raise the cost of a violation. Stale evidence raises uncertainty. Contradictions demand inspection. Expensive actions make guessing less acceptable. The score is not a model prediction; it is a teaching instrument for making hidden tradeoffs visible.

01 / ProtectPreserve the strongest invariant when the action could cause durable harm.
02 / InspectWhen evidence and rules disagree, gather the smallest new observation that can resolve it.
03 / ResumeContinue only after the reason to trust the next step is stronger than the cost of being wrong.
