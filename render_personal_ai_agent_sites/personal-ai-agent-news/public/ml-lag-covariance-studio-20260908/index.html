Validity is a joint constraint

With centered observations y, the lag-k numerator is the sum of y(i) y(i+k) over the n-k available pairs. The fixed-denominator estimate divides every numerator by n. The adjusted estimate divides by n-k.

A covariance matrix must have nonnegative quadratic forms. The fixed-denominator Toeplitz matrix is a Gram matrix of zero-padded shifts, divided by n, so it is positive semidefinite. Separately correcting each lag can destroy that property. The displayed unit eigenvector of the adjusted matrix gives its smallest quadratic form.

With a known zero mean and stationary observations, the pair-count normalization is unbiased at each lag. Subtracting an estimated sample mean changes that claim. Neither label guarantees a better forecast. This eight-point experiment demonstrates matrix validity, not estimator accuracy on a population.
