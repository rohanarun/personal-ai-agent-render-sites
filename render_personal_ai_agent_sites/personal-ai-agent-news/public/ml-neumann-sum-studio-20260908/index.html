The telescoping certificate

For Sₙ = Σⱼ₌₀ᴺFʲ, multiplication gives (I − F)Sₙ = I − Fᴺ⁺¹. Thus the residual is exactly Fᴺ⁺¹, and the inverse error is (I − F)⁻¹Fᴺ⁺¹. Residual and inverse error are different quantities.

When q = ‖F‖∞ < 1, summing the geometric tail gives ‖(I − F)⁻¹ − Sₙ‖∞ ≤ qᴺ⁺¹/(1 − q). Here ‖·‖∞ means the largest absolute row sum, not the largest entry.

A sufficient test can fail

The family F = [[r, s], [0, r]] has ‖F‖∞ = |r| + s. For j ≥ 1 its diagonal is rʲ and upper entry is jsrʲ⁻¹. Because |r| < 1 throughout this experiment, both terms decay and the series converges even when q ≥ 1. The unavailable bound is not evidence of divergence.

At r = 0, F² = 0, so I + F is already the exact inverse. With negative r, terms alternate; a partial sum need not approach the inverse monotonically entry by entry. Inverse-series approximations arise in numerical linear solves and sensitivity calculations, but this tiny example is not a scalable solver recommendation.
