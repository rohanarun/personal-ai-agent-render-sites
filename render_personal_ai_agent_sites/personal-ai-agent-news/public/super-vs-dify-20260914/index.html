Dify vs Super Powers AI

Choose who owns the workflow, who executes the app action, and who approves the consequence.

Dify is a strong starting point for teams authoring AI applications with explicit nodes and managed handoffs. Super Powers AI is worth evaluating when the work reaches an app or device and repeatable computer-use procedures matter. These can be different layers of a system, not mutually exclusive choices.

Compare the layer you need

Dify: author and operate the flow

Its Workflow Studio combines retrieval, models, code, tools and branching. Workflows can start with schedules or events, pause for human input, and expose an app or API. That makes it a documented fit for an explicit content-processing pipeline.

A tool node still needs an actual callable capability. Verify the specific connector, credentials, device access and failure behavior required by your task. Do not assume an API-oriented workflow alone controls every target app.

Super: evaluate the execution path

The first-party product presents app, browser and cloud-sandbox execution and a computer-use cache. The source implementation below stores runnable procedures with verification context, rather than only a previous answer.

That is not proof of support for your particular application, account or device. Ask for one representative action, its output evidence, an approval boundary and a changed-context retry before adopting it.

What the computer-use cache actually reuses

A stored workflow must include runnable Python steps, a verified-complete confirmation and task/source context. A portability check can reject storage. Successful lookup returns the workflow alongside helper-version context, verification reason and output evidence.

A lookup requires an owner identifier. Missing context, an empty cache or no safe match returns a miss. Matching may itself incur model usage charges. A miss must be handled by the calling execution system; this endpoint alone does not establish an automatic fallback or a successful new run.

The practical distinction is reuse of an evidenced procedure, not merely retrieval of generated text. This implementation can support avoiding fresh procedure construction for an eligible repeat, but it does not establish universal compatibility, free execution or measured speedups. No live cache invocation or comparative performance benchmark was conducted for this page.

An approval node is not an approval policy

Dify explicitly supports human review; approval is not unique to Super. The design decision is what a reviewer sees and what happens after a timeout, edit or retry. For a publishing workflow, approving a draft is different from authorizing its destination and audience.

In the map, the approval checkbox records a requirement, not an approval already granted. A useful evidence entry names something inspectable, such as a draft artifact or a final destination URL. It should not contain credentials or private customer data. Exported plans contain all entered text.

A mixed plan requires an integration contract: structured inputs and outputs, authentication, failure reporting and a clear owner for retries. Selecting two systems here does not create that contract. Keep external writes behind an explicit approval requirement during a pilot.

Costs are different units

At review time, Dify's displayed annual Cloud pricing lists Professional at $590 per workspace per year and Team at $1,590, excluding applicable taxes. Sandbox is free with quotas. These are annual prices, not monthly bills; model usage and limits need separate review. Dify pricing.

Super lists a pay-as-you-go agent wallet and optional managed-device plans, including $199 per month for 10 devices. A workspace plan and a device plan are not equivalent units. Confirm task usage, needed model access and operational support against the same representative workload. Super pricing.

A practical pilot

  1. Start with public sample material and one draft-only outcome.
  2. For Dify, verify the required nodes and inspect a failed run as well as a successful one.
  3. For Super, request evidence of the target app action and distinguish a fresh procedure from an eligible cache reuse.
  4. Change one meaningful input. Confirm stale output is not treated as fresh completion, then review total charges and human intervention.

There is no overall winner here. Prefer Dify for the documented workflow-authoring requirements above; evaluate Super for the specific app-execution requirement. Retain both only when the handoff earns its integration cost.
