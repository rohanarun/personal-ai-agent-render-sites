Order matters inside the exponential

The Frechet derivative L(A,E) is the linear term in exp(A + tE). It integrates all placements of E between exponential factors. When A and E commute, those factors combine and L(A,E) = exp(A)E.

L(A,E) = integral_0^1 exp((1-s)A) E exp(sA) ds
exp(A+tE) = exp(A) + t L(A,E) + O(t squared)

Here A = diag(a,-a) and E is a rotated diag(1,-1). Integrating entrywise gives diagonal multipliers exp(a), exp(-a), and off-diagonal multiplier sinh(a)/a, with limit 1 at a = 0.

ml-matrix symmetric eigendecomposition evaluates each exponential. The derivative is the analytic integral for this family. The scalar-style shortcut is intentionally shown as a counterexample, not a general algorithm. Even the correct linear prediction has a finite-step remainder.
