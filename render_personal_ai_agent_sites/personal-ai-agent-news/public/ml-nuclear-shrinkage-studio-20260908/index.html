The nuclear norm is the sum of singular values, not the sum of absolute matrix entries. For A = U diag(sigma) VT, the unique minimizer of F is U diag(max(sigma - tau, 0)) VT. This is the proximal map of the nuclear norm.

Hard truncation here sets values at or below tau to zero and leaves larger values untouched. It minimizes approximation error at its retained rank, but generally does not minimize the nuclear-norm-penalized objective. The soft solution accepts extra fitting error in exchange for a smaller penalty.

Watch a direction disappear

For the uneven spectrum at tau = 1, soft thresholding gives [2, 0.5, 0], while hard truncation gives [3, 1.5, 0]. Both have rank two. Rotating the left basis changes their entries but preserves all singular values, Frobenius errors and nuclear norms.

At tau = 0 the soft result is A. At or above the largest singular value it becomes zero. At a crossing, the soft result approaches zero continuously in that direction; the hard result jumps. With tied singular values, the selected singular vectors are not unique, but uniform soft thresholding gives the same reconstructed matrix.

This is a fully observed proximal problem, not an iterative matrix-completion algorithm or proof of improved denoising. ml-matrix supplies the SVD. Numerical rank counts singular values above 1e-10; hard-threshold equality uses the same tolerance.
