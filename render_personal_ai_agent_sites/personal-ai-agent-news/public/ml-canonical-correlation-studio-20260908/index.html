Find the relationship, not just an axis

Canonical correlation analysis chooses one linear combination from X and another from Y to maximize their correlation. The next pair is constrained to be uncorrelated with the previous scores within each view. Unlike PCA, its objective is a relationship between two views, not variance inside one view.

This implementation whitens both sample covariance matrices, then takes an SVD of the whitened cross-covariance. Its singular values are the canonical correlations. The reported weights give sample variance one to each score. Weight signs can flip together without changing their correlation.

A controlled eight-row experiment

Four centered, mutually orthogonal sign columns form z1, z2, e1, and e2. Before rotation, X = (z1, z2) and Y = (r1 z1 + sqrt(1-r1 squared) e1, r2 z2 + sqrt(1-r2 squared) e2). Rotations preserve the information in each view, so the canonical correlations remain the sorted latent correlations.

These are designed observations, not a random sample or evidence of causality. Some plotted observations can coincide. Equal canonical correlations make individual directions non-unique. Zero linear cross-correlation does not imply statistical independence. Real high-dimensional CCA also needs held-out validation and may require regularization; this small full-rank demonstration does neither.
