Start with the decision, then collect evidence

Suppose you sell a data integration product. Your question is not “tell me everything about this account.” It is “what evidence would make a discovery conversation about integration relevant?” Give the research task a boundary: the company's own public pages, a defined time window, and internal records you are authorized to use.

  1. Match the organization. Confirm the domain and entity. A similarly named company or a subsidiary's announcement is not automatically evidence about your target.
  2. Record the observation. Keep a source URL or approved internal reference, the publication date when available, and the date you checked it. Separate the page's words from your summary.
  3. Write the narrow implication. A job listing that mentions a technology may justify a question about it. It does not prove the technology is deployed across the company.
  4. Write one discovery question. Ask what changed, who owns the workflow or whether a constraint still applies. Leave unverified budget and purchasing intent out of the facts.

A brief should still be useful when the answer is “not enough evidence.” That result prevents irrelevant outreach and gives the next researcher an explicit gap to investigate.

From a source to a conversation

The following Northline account is fictional. These sample observations demonstrate the distinction between evidence and an inference; they are not claims about a real company. Nothing here researches a prospect, updates a CRM or sends outreach.

Careers page / checked today

A listing mentions PostgreSQL experience

Supported: the listing asks for that experience.

Not established: the production database, migration plans or purchase budget.

Question: “Which data systems does this team own?”

Company announcement / this month

A second regional office is planned

Supported: the company announced a plan.

Not established: the office is open or integration work is funded.

Question: “Does the expansion change how teams share operational data?”

Internal note / last year

An integration project was paused

Supported: a past note recorded a pause.

Not established: the current status, owner or reason.

Question: “Is that project still paused, or has the scope changed?”

    Give Super a bounded research assignment

    Use this as a proposed pilot brief, adapted to the permissions and sources you actually have. It describes an evaluation task, not a promise that every source or application is supported.

    Research one approved account for one discovery call. Use its official pages and the approved redacted internal notes. Return source statements with references and observation dates, hypotheses in a separate section, unresolved conflicts, and three questions. Do not infer budget or intent from hiring or expansion alone. Stop at inaccessible sources; do not bypass access controls. Do not send messages or change CRM records.

    Review the result against the original sources before using it. A missing source should remain missing. A source that contradicts another should remain a conflict until resolved. If Clay already produces the brief you need, adding Super has to earn its place through a demonstrated improvement on the actual task.

    Measure the complete research loop

    Compare the same small set of redacted accounts, including ambiguous names and stale pages. Record correct source attribution, unsupported claims, unanswered questions, researcher correction time and total cost. Include failed runs and review time. We have not run that comparison, so this article claims no accuracy advantage, revenue lift or time saving.

    What the computer-use cache actually reuses

    Super's public computer-use-cache project is inspectable software: an OpenAI-compatible proxy that can store successful JSON responses and reuse them for matching canonical requests. Cache misses go upstream; expiry and bypass controls are present. This is response reuse, not model training or automatic replay of a completed sales workflow.

    In the reviewed implementation, model, messages, tools and most body fields participate in the key. Metadata is excluded by default. Put changing source content in the actual request context, not only in excluded metadata. Do not remove important differences just to increase cache hits.

    A hit can avoid an upstream model call; it does not confirm that a job listing is still live or that a company announcement is current. Fetch current evidence through the approved workflow, preserve the observation time, and treat a reused response as dependent on its input. No measured end-to-end savings are claimed here.

    Pricing: match the units to the workflow

    Clay's pricing page separates actions from data credits and offers Free, Launch, Growth and Enterprise plans. Free currently lists 500 actions and 100 data credits per month; paid allowances and billing selections vary. Check the live configuration rather than comparing a single scraped dollar figure across different bundles. Clay pricing, checked September 15, 2026

    For Super, include model usage, work-surface charges and human review. A cache hit is not equivalent to an enriched account or a completed brief. The right comparison is the total cost of an accepted, source-checked result at your required freshness, not one vendor's credit versus another's model call. Super product and pricing information

    The practical answer

    For repeatable account enrichment and delivery, evaluate Clay first. For a particular research gap across authorized work surfaces, test Super Powers AI with a short evidence-first assignment. Keep the customer conversation human-reviewed, and let unsupported conclusions become questions.
