Why infinity appears

In weighted split conformal prediction, the calibration residuals and a point at infinity share one normalized weight distribution. The interval uses its first quantile reaching the target coverage. Omitting the test mass changes that distribution.

Follow the calculation
  1. Keep the fitted predictor separate from the calibration examples. Here its test prediction is fixed at zero.
  2. Add all calibration weights W and the test weight w. Divide every weight by W + w.
  3. Accumulate normalized calibration weights in residual order. The first residual reaching the target is q.
  4. If the finite mass is below the target, q is infinite and the prediction set is the entire real line.

q = Quantile(1 - alpha, sum p_i delta_r_i + p_test delta_infinity)

When does it become unbounded?

For positive finite weights, the finite mass is W/(W+w). Infinity is selected when w/(W+w) > alpha. At equality, the largest calibration residual reaches the target and the interval is finite.

What the guarantee assumes

The covariate-shift result assumes an unchanged conditional response distribution and appropriate density-ratio weights. Arbitrary or estimated weights do not automatically inherit the exact guarantee. This fixed six-point example explains the quantile; it does not estimate empirical coverage or demonstrate conditional coverage.
