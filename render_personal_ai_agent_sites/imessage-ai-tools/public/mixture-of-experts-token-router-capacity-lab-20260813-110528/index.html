Scale parameters without paying for all of them.

A dense layer runs every parameter for every token. An MoE router scores experts, selects the top few, and combines their outputs. The hard part is not selection alone: capacity, overflow, and balancing pressure decide whether experts specialize or collapse into a traffic jam.

Inside the layer

Score. Dispatch. Combine.

Router logits

A small learned projection scores each token against every expert. Softmax turns those logits into routing weights.

p(expert | token) = softmax(Wᵣx)

Sparse dispatch

Only the top-k experts receive the token. Their outputs are weighted by router probability and added back to the residual stream.

y = Σ pᵢ · Expertᵢ(x)

Auxiliary balance

A balancing objective discourages every token from choosing the same specialist. It trades a little routing freedom for stable utilization.

Laux ∝ expert fraction × mean gate

Capacity

Each expert has a bounded token buffer. Too little capacity saves memory but creates overflow; too much wastes padded computation.

Specialization

Experts can become unusually good at code, names, syntax, or languages, even though those roles emerge from routing pressure rather than labels.

Collapse

Without balancing, a popular expert attracts more gradient signal, improves faster, and can become even more popular. The feedback loop starves peers.
