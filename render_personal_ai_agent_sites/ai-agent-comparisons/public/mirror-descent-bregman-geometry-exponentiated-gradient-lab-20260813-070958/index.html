The mirror step

Measure distance with the potential, not a ruler.

Mirror descent linearizes the objective, then regularizes movement with a Bregman divergence generated by a strictly convex potential. The potential encodes the constraint’s natural geometry.

Take the gradient step in dual coordinates

The mirror map sends the feasible point through ∇ψ. The loss gradient subtracts directly in this unconstrained dual representation.

∇ψ(xₜ₊₁) = ∇ψ(xₜ) − ηgₜ

Map back to the feasible set

The inverse mirror map returns a point whose geometry respects nonnegativity, normalization, or another constraint.

Euclidean potential

Squared distance makes additive steps natural, followed by projection onto the simplex.

ψ(x)=½||x||²

Negative entropy

KL-like Bregman divergence makes ratios natural and produces multiplicative exponentiated-gradient updates.

Log barrier

The potential explodes near zero, strongly resisting the boundary while preserving interior feasibility.

ψ(x)=−Σ log xᵢ
Constraint consequences

The boundary remembers how you arrived.

The same gradient can mean an additive displacement, a multiplicative ratio, or an interior barrier response. Those choices become visible when an expert’s probability nears zero.

Projection can create exact zeros.

A large Euclidean step may leave the simplex. Projection restores feasibility by shifting and clipping coordinates; a weak expert can land exactly on the boundary.

ΠΔ

Entropy preserves positive support.

Exponentiated gradient multiplies each positive weight by an exponential factor. It can become tiny, but never reaches exact zero in finite arithmetic before clipping.

e−ηg

Barriers become stiff near the edge.

As a coordinate approaches zero, the barrier curvature grows. The method stays interior, but numerical steps can become conservative and sensitive to solver accuracy.

−log x

Choose a potential that fits the domain.

Entropy is natural for distributions and multiplicative scale. Euclidean geometry is natural for unconstrained vectors. Matrix domains invite spectral or quantum entropy mirrors.

Support behavior is an algorithmic decision.

Add a probability floor when exploration must remain possible. Without it, projected updates can permanently suppress experts under future additive steps.

Tune by regret and computational budget.

Compare learning-rate sweeps, projection cost, numerical precision, support size, and adversarial sequences rather than one convenient loss stream.

Evaluation lens
“A small step depends on the geometry.”

Euclidean distance treats equal coordinate changes alike. Entropy geometry measures relative changes and becomes sensitive near the simplex boundary.
