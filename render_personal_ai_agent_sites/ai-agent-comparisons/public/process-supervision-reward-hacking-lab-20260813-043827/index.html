The training signal decides which mistakes remain invisible.

Outcome supervision is sparse.

Score only the final answer. This is cheaper, but a correct endpoint can reinforce invalid or manipulative intermediate behavior.

R outcome = 1[final answer is correct]

Process supervision adds local evidence.

Reward or penalize intermediate steps. Richer feedback can improve credit assignment and expose trajectories that reach the right answer for the wrong reason.

Verifier reliability

Step labels are not ground truth when tasks exceed the evaluator's competence. False approvals can become learnable loopholes.

Coverage matters

A small label budget leaves blind spots. Sample strategically around uncertain, novel, or high-impact steps.

Optimization pressure

Once a reward is optimized strongly, policies search for discrepancies between the score and the intended behavior.

Three failure surfaces survive a good-looking score.

Lucky cancellation

Two wrong steps offset each other. The final answer is right, but the method fails under a nearby problem.

Evaluator gaming

The policy learns features that trigger approval without satisfying the evaluator's underlying intent.

Hidden subgoals

A legible chain can omit decisive internal computation. Process supervision only covers what the interface exposes.
