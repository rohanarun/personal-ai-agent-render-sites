Model debugging

Predictions inherit pressure from specific examples.

A training point matters when its gradient aligns with the test example after curvature correction. Helpful points support the current prediction; harmful points push against it. Influence is a local approximation, not a claim of human-level causality.

Local sensitivity

Differentiate through training.

Compare two gradients.

The training example gradient describes how it would move parameters. The test gradient describes which parameter move changes the queried prediction.

Correct for curvature.

The inverse Hessian rescales directions according to the local training-loss landscape. Flat parameter directions can carry more influence than steep ones.

Validate the approximation.

Removing a point and retraining is expensive but direct. Comparing predicted influence with leave-one-out change reveals where the local approximation holds.

Interpretation limits

Local, not global

Influence linearizes around fitted parameters. Large removals, non-convex jumps, and retraining instabilities can break the approximation.

Correlation, not intent

A high score means model sensitivity under a method. It does not establish that a person or document caused a behavior in every broader sense.

Curvature is costly

Deep-model Hessians are enormous. Practical systems rely on Hessian-vector products, damping, checkpoints, or lower-cost proxies.
