The central bargain

Fit is evidence. Complexity is the price.

A posterior can hug a low-error solution, but certainty costs KL divergence. A broad posterior stays closer to the prior, yet may make more mistakes. PAC-Bayes turns both pressures into a high-probability statement about unseen data.

Bound ≈ empirical risk + √(( KL(Q‖P) + confidence cost ) / sample size)

Read the certificate

Three forces decide whether the bound says anything.

Evidence dilutes complexity.

With more independent examples, the same KL cost is spread across a larger evidence base. Watch the penalty contract roughly with the inverse square root of sample size.

n

The prior sets the reference.

KL divergence measures how surprising the learned posterior is relative to a data-independent prior. Moving means, shrinking variances, and adding effective dimensions all increase the bill.

KL

Confidence is not free.

Demanding a smaller failure probability raises the confidence cost. A useful report states the bound, its confidence level, its prior, and the assumptions behind the sample.

δ

Failure modes

Vacuous certificate

If the upper bound reaches one, it cannot rule out total failure. More data, a better prior, or a less concentrated posterior may help.

Data-dependent prior

A prior chosen after seeing the same training data can invalidate the simple guarantee unless the analysis explicitly pays for that dependence.

Wrong sampling story

The theorem's confidence is only as meaningful as its assumptions. Distribution shift and correlated samples need separate treatment.
