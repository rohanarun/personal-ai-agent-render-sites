Evaluation needs provenance.

A benchmark estimates generalization on unseen items. Pretraining can include exact test questions or close paraphrases. Memorization raises observed accuracy without raising transferable capability. Decontamination changes the denominator and the claim.

Inspect items, not only averages.

Exact matching catches copies but misses translated, reordered, or paraphrased test items. Strong audits combine lexical, semantic, temporal, and behavioral evidence.

Four signals make a stronger contamination claim.

String overlap

N-gram and exact-match searches provide direct evidence, but are brittle under formatting and paraphrase changes.

Semantic similarity

Embedding or model-based matching finds meaning-preserving rewrites, while also creating false positives among naturally similar questions.

Temporal provenance

Release dates, crawl snapshots, and training cutoffs can prove that some knowledge could not have entered through the claimed corpus.

Behavioral evidence

Unusual likelihood, completion, or perturbation patterns can support an exposure hypothesis, but rarely identify the source alone.
