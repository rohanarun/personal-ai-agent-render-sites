A one-model ensemble

Average parameters, not predictions.

Conventional training returns the final iterate near the edge of a good region. SWA keeps a running arithmetic mean of late checkpoints. When those checkpoints share a connected basin, their mean can move toward a wider, more stable solution without the inference cost of an ensemble.

Why the mean can win

Geometry turns checkpoints into a better answer.

SGD keeps moving.

A cyclical or high constant learning rate collects diverse, high-performing points around a connected low-loss region instead of collapsing immediately onto one edge.

SWA estimates the center.

The arithmetic mean of selected weights often sits in a broader part of the basin. It is still one network at inference time, so prediction cost stays near the SGD baseline.

SWAG models local shape.

The checkpoint mean and covariance define a Gaussian approximation in weight space. Sampling models from it supports approximate Bayesian model averaging and uncertainty estimates.
