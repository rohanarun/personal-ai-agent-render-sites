Four answers to one counterfactual.

Each estimator asks what the target policy would have earned using data collected by a different behavior policy. Their failure modes differ: model bias, weight variance, finite-sample normalization, and missing support.

Importance sampling changes the measure.

Each observed reward is reweighted by how much more or less likely the target policy was to choose that logged action.

w_i = pi_target(a_i | x_i) / pi_behavior(a_i | x_i)

A few weights can own the estimate.

Low overlap creates heavy tails. Effective sample size exposes when 600 rows behave like 12.

Direct method

Predict both action outcomes and average under the target policy. Stable, but biased when the reward model is wrong.

V_DM = mean_x sum_a pi(a|x) q_hat(x,a)

Self-normalized IPS

Divide weighted rewards by total weight. This often reduces variance while introducing finite-sample bias.

V_SNIPS = sum_i w_i r_i / sum_i w_i

Doubly robust

Start with the direct estimate, then correct its logged-action residual with an importance weight.

V_DR = mean_i [q_hat_pi(x_i) + w_i(r_i-q_hat(x_i,a_i))]

The estimator cannot repair absent evidence.

Overlap is an identification condition.

If the target policy chooses actions the behavior policy almost never took, logged data contain little evidence for those counterfactuals. Large weights are the numerical symptom; no estimator can manufacture the missing outcomes.

Clipping trades variance for bias.

Capping importance weights prevents one rare action from dominating a finite sample. It can stabilize an estimate, but it changes the estimand unless the clipped region contributes negligibly.

Doubly robust does not mean invulnerable.

The estimator is consistent when either the propensity model or outcome model is correct under regularity conditions. When propensities are wrong and the outcome model is misspecified, both defenses can fail together.

Design logs for evaluation, not just operations.

Propensities
Log the probability, not only the action.

Store the exact behavior-policy probability for the chosen action at decision time. Reconstructing it later from a changed model breaks the weight ratio.

Exploration
Preserve bounded support.

A small randomized exploration floor can make future policy comparisons possible, subject to product safety and user-consent constraints.

Uncertainty
Report intervals and ESS.

A point estimate can look precise while depending on a handful of weighted rows. Pair confidence procedures with weight diagnostics and subgroup checks.

Deployment
Use staged online confirmation.

Off-policy evaluation is a gate, not a final verdict. Confirm promising policies with guarded rollout, monitoring, and a rollback path.

Read the estimate like an investigator.

"A high row count is not a high effective sample size."

Importance weights determine how much independent evidence the target policy actually receives.
