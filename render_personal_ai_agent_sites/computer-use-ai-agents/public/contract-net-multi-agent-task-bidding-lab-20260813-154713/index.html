The conversation contract

Five acts create one commitment.

Contract Net is not just a ranking algorithm. Its message sequence tells each participant what it may do next and when a proposal becomes binding.

CFP
Manager → participants

Announce

Describe the task, evaluation conditions, conversation identifier, and reply-by deadline.

P/R
Participants → manager

Propose or refuse

Capable agents expose a price, completion estimate, and confidence. Others explicitly refuse.

A/R
Manager → participants

Award and reject

Score valid proposals, accept the winner, and close every losing proposal with a rejection.

I/F
Contractor → manager

Inform or fail

The accepted bidder executes under commitment, then reports a result or a failure.

Coordination has a price.

Decentralized bidding reveals local capability without a global scheduler, but deadlines, message volume, and strategic scoring shape the outcome.

Fast replies are not always strong proposals. If high-skill agents have longer communication or deliberation delays, their bids arrive late and the manager optimizes over a biased subset.

Sending CFPs only to likely-capable agents cuts messages and refusals. It can also hide an unexpectedly good bidder, so the simulation filters on observed task affinity rather than perfect knowledge.

The manager shortlists strong proposals and requests a final bid. This adds messages and latency, but allows contractors to sharpen price and time after learning they remain competitive.
